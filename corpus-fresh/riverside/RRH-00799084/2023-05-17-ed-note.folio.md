## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Cough and shortness of breath.

**History.** The patient came in with several days of cough, tiredness and low appetite, with a low-grade fever at home. There has been no chest pain. Allergy list: lisinopril (cough). Home medications are listed in the chart and have not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:18: BP 111/67, HR 84, RR 18, temperature 99.2 F, SpO2 95%. Alert and conversing in full sentences. Mild crackles at the right lung base, no wheeze. Heart regular. Abdomen soft and nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="cbc">White blood cells 14.9, hemoglobin 13.5, platelets 246</ref> on the 14:30 panel. Basic metabolic panel from the same draw: sodium 132, potassium 4.2, chloride 101, bicarbonate 19, anion gap 12, urea nitrogen 56, creatinine 1.48, eGFR 45, glucose 127. The kidney function is reduced and the sodium is low; we gave fluids as below.

**ED course.** Normal saline at 75 mL/h was started at 15:02 and is still running. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 15:42.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>, based on the cough, the exam findings at the right base and the raised white count. Oxygen saturation is acceptable without supplemental oxygen. Treat with ceftriaxone and admit to the hospitalist service for further care.
2. Reduced kidney function with an elevated urea nitrogen; continue IV fluids and recheck the panel in the morning. The hospitalist will address the home medications on admission.

Discussed with the patient, who agrees with admission.
