## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient came to the emergency department this evening with chest pain. The discomfort was not pleasant but was tolerable, and it did not change with exertion during the visit. There was no fainting. The allergy list reads NKDA. The home medication list was reviewed but not reconciled during this visit: metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, lisinopril 40 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily, and amlodipine 5 mg daily.

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:54: BP 162/97, HR 78, RR 23, T 98.3 F, SpO2 95%. The patient was comfortable at rest, speaking in full sentences. Heart rhythm regular, lungs clear, chest wall without reproducible pain on my exam, and no leg swelling.

**Results.** An ECG was obtained on arrival and showed no ischemic changes. The first troponin was drawn at 20:13: <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The repeat was drawn at 23:13: <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 20:13 showed sodium 141, potassium 4.6, chloride 105, bicarbonate 26, anion gap 10, urea nitrogen 16, creatinine 1.39 (H), eGFR 52 (L), and glucose 123 (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_acs">Chest pain, acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>. Acute coronary syndrome ruled out: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The patient's pain was not accompanied by any abnormal vital-sign trend other than the elevated blood pressure noted on arrival, and the patient remained stable throughout the stay.
2. Blood pressure was 162/97 at triage. This should be rechecked by the primary care physician at follow-up.
3. Creatinine 1.39 with eGFR 52 on the 20:13 panel; the primary care physician should follow this on outpatient labs.

The results were discussed with the patient, who understood them and was reassured. Return precautions were reviewed: return for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or any new concerning symptom. Follow up with the primary care physician within the week.
