## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented with several days of cough, a low-grade fever, fatigue and poor oral intake. No chest pain. They had not been able to keep up with fluids at home. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:54: BP 132/79, HR 97, RR 17, T 99.2 F, SpO2 97%. Appears tired but is in no distress. Mucous membranes are mildly dry. Lung exam shows crackles at the base, with no wheeze. Heart is regular. Abdomen is soft and nontender.

**Results.** Labs were drawn at 17:12. The CBC shows <ref id="cbc">white blood cells 14.2, hemoglobin 13.7 and platelets 335</ref>. The basic metabolic panel shows sodium 133, potassium 4.4, chloride 101, bicarbonate 21, anion gap 11, urea nitrogen 56, creatinine 2.27, eGFR 31 and glucose 143. The chest radiograph findings fit the diagnosis below.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 18:18 and is running.
2. Kidney injury with elevated urea nitrogen and creatinine, consistent with volume depletion. Normal saline at 75 mL/h was started at 17:38 and is running.
3. Home medications: metformin and empagliflozin are held while she is acutely ill and the kidney function is reduced. Losartan 100 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly are listed to continue.
4. Disposition: admit to the hospitalist service for continued IV antibiotics and fluids. The plan was discussed with the patient, who agrees.
