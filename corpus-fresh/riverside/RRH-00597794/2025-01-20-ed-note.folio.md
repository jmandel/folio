## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is a woman with type 2 diabetes and hypertension who came in with three days of vomiting and loose stools, followed by worsening weakness, abdominal discomfort and fast breathing today. She has eaten almost nothing and has been sipping water. No fever at home, no chest pain. She takes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> for her diabetes. Her allergy list includes lisinopril (angioedema).

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:11: BP 102/61, HR 96, RR 16, T 98.7 F, SpO2 96%. She looks tired and dry, with dry mucous membranes. Breathing is deep but unlabored. Abdomen is soft with mild diffuse tenderness and no guarding. She is alert and oriented.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 14:20: sodium 134, potassium 4.8, chloride 93, bicarbonate 10 (critical), anion gap 31, urea nitrogen 36, creatinine 1.46, eGFR 57, glucose 157.</ref> Venous blood gas on the same draw: pH 7.21, pCO2 24. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.2 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, with a wide anion gap, low bicarbonate and a markedly elevated ketone level despite a glucose of 157. This is in keeping with her SGLT2 inhibitor use in the setting of poor intake. An IV insulin infusion per DKA protocol is being started at 0.1 units/kg/h, with fluids and close lab monitoring.
2. Acute kidney injury pattern from volume loss; creatinine 1.46 to be rechecked with the next panel.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for ongoing care. Her home medications have not yet been reconciled and will be addressed by the admitting team.
