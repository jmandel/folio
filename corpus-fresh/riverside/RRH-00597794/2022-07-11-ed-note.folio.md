## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is a middle-aged man with hypertension and type 2 diabetes who came in this evening because his lips and tongue had been swelling over the past few hours. He describes a thick feeling in his mouth and some difficulty with his words, but no hives, no itching, no wheezing and no trouble swallowing his saliva. Nothing new in his diet and no insect bites. He takes his blood pressure medicine every morning and has been on it for a long time. He reports no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 22:11: BP 146/88, HR 103, RR 23, T 98.3 F, SpO2 97% on room air. He is anxious but speaking in full sentences. There is visible swelling of the lower lip and the tongue, without a rash. The uvula is midline and the airway is patent with no stridor. Lungs are clear. Heart is regular and mildly fast.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling is isolated to the lips and tongue without hives, which fits a bradykinin-mediated process rather than an allergic one. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given at 22:30. He is on a monitor with the airway checked repeatedly, and anesthesia is aware in case the swelling progresses. He will be observed in the hospital overnight.
2. Home medication: <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 40 mg daily is stopped, and he was told not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. He and his wife both understand this, and it will be recorded prominently in his chart.

I will reassess the airway again within the hour and hand off to the admitting team.
