## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, malaise and rapid breathing.

**History.** The patient presented this evening feeling unwell and was noted to be breathing fast. She takes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>. Her other home medications are metformin 500 mg twice daily, losartan 50 mg daily, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly. None have been reconciled yet in the ED. She has no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** At 18:26: BP 105/63, HR 95, RR 20, T 98.2 F, SpO2 95%. She appears mildly dry and uncomfortable, with deep respirations. The abdomen is soft.

**Results.** Venous blood gas at 18:45: pH 7.22, pCO2 28. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.5. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 18:45: sodium 132, potassium 5.1, chloride 98, bicarbonate 14, anion gap 20, BUN 28, creatinine 1.14, eGFR 64, glucose 192.</ref>

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: pH 7.22, bicarbonate 14 and an elevated anion gap with a glucose of only 192 and a beta-hydroxybutyrate of 5.5. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h is ordered to start at 19:30, with dextrose-containing fluids.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> as a cause of the presentation, still being worked up.
3. The hospitalist team will be called to admit her.
