## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, vomiting and shortness of breath.

**History.** The patient presents with several days of vomiting and poor oral intake and now feels weak and short of breath. He has type 2 diabetes and takes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> at home, among other medications. He has no known drug allergies. Pain was not a major complaint, though he noted some upper abdominal discomfort.

**Exam.** Vitals at 15:01: BP 118/71, HR 96, RR 23, T 98.4 F, SpO2 96%. He appears ill and dehydrated, with dry mucous membranes and deep, rapid breathing. Abdomen is soft with mild upper tenderness and no guarding. He is alert and oriented.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn at 15:10 shows pH 7.10 and pCO2 21. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 7.1. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel on the 15:10 draw: sodium 134, potassium 5.5, chloride 92, bicarbonate 13, anion gap 29, urea nitrogen 33, creatinine 1.27, eGFR 66, glucose 219.</ref>

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a wide anion gap acidosis with a markedly elevated ketone level despite a glucose of only 219. Begin an insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h, with intravenous fluids. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given the upper abdominal discomfort; lipase is pending at the time of this note.
3. Potassium is elevated at 5.5; it will be rechecked as insulin is given.

Case discussed with the hospitalist, who accepts the patient for admission.
