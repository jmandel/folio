## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the emergency department with cough and feeling unwell and was evaluated for a possible chest infection. No known drug allergies. Home medications are losartan 100 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly; these have not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 13:26: BP 111/67, HR 111, RR 23, T 98.1 F, SpO2 97%. The patient looks tired but is alert and conversant. Breathing is mildly quick, and the chest exam is consistent with a lung infection. Abdomen is soft.

**Results.** Blood drawn at 13:48. <ref id="cbc">White blood cells 16.8 (H), hemoglobin 12.3 (L), platelets 252</ref>. Basic metabolic panel: sodium 134 (L), potassium 4.3, chloride 100, bicarbonate 16 (L), anion gap 18 (H), urea nitrogen 47 (H), creatinine 1.75 (H), eGFR 43 (L), glucose 154 (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 14:50. The white count is elevated and the heart rate was fast on arrival.
2. Fluids: normal saline 0.9% at 75 mL/h started 14:10 and is still running. The kidney and electrolyte values above are noted and will be followed with repeat labs.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for continued treatment. Care was discussed with the admitting physician.
