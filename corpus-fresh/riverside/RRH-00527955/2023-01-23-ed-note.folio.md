## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with several days of feeling unwell, nausea, and poor appetite, and has been taking little by mouth. There was no chest pain. Home medications were reviewed on arrival: <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily, not yet reconciled</ref>. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:40: BP 98/59, HR 85, RR 17, T 99.2 F, SpO2 99%. The patient appears fatigued and mildly dry. Breathing is unlabored. The abdomen is soft, with mild diffuse discomfort and no guarding or rebound.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 22:08 shows sodium 132, potassium 4.9, chloride 92, bicarbonate 11 (critical), anion gap 29, urea nitrogen 34, creatinine 1.70, eGFR 45, and glucose 228</ref>. Venous blood gas drawn at 22:08: pH 7.16 (critical), pCO2 22. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.0 mmol/L. The glucose is only modestly elevated despite a profound anion gap acidosis.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The wide anion gap, low bicarbonate, acidemic venous pH and markedly elevated ketones establish the diagnosis even though the glucose is near 228. The insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 22:44 and is running. The patient is being admitted to the hospitalist service for continued care and close monitoring.
2. Because of the abdominal discomfort and the acidosis, <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">pancreatitis is still being ruled out</ref> and remains a working consideration while the evaluation continues.
3. Renal function is worse than expected, with creatinine 1.70; this will be followed on repeat panels.

The patient and family were updated on the findings and the need for admission.
