## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the ED on the evening of 5/6 with chest pain. The pain was evaluated on arrival with an ECG, laboratory studies and serial troponins. Allergies: NKDA. Home medications as listed in the chart, not yet reconciled at the time of this note: metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, dapagliflozin 10 mg daily, insulin glargine 10 units subcutaneously nightly, lisinopril 20 mg daily, hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily, and levothyroxine 75 mcg daily.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:01: BP 164/98, HR 109, RR 23, T 99 F, SpO2 99%. The patient was alert and conversant, in no acute distress at rest, and speaking in full sentences. Heart rhythm was regular, though the rate was fast on arrival. Lungs were clear and breathing was unlabored. The abdomen was soft and nontender.

**Results.** The ECG showed no ischemic changes. The first troponin, drawn at 23:23, was <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The repeat, drawn at 02:23 on 5/7, was <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The basic metabolic panel from the 23:23 draw showed sodium 141 mmol/L, potassium 4.5 mmol/L, chloride 102 mmol/L, bicarbonate 28 mmol/L, anion gap 11 mmol/L, urea nitrogen 19 mg/dL, creatinine 1.48 mg/dL (H), eGFR 39 mL/min/1.73m2 (L) and glucose 129 mg/dL (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The blood pressure and heart rate were elevated at triage, and I explained to the patient that the cardiac work-up is reassuring.
2. Creatinine 1.48 with eGFR 39 on the 23:23 panel; electrolytes and bicarbonate are otherwise unremarkable. The home medication list is still to be reconciled.
3. Follow-up with the primary care physician in the coming days to review blood pressure and the kidney function result. The patient should return for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting.
