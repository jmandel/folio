## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the ED on the evening of 6/4 with acute chest pain. The pain was evaluated on arrival with an ECG, laboratory studies and serial cardiac markers. No known drug allergies are listed. Home medications on the list are metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, dapagliflozin 10 mg daily and lisinopril 20 mg daily; these have not been reconciled at this visit.

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:11: BP 151/91, HR 89, RR 18, temperature 98.8 F, SpO2 96%. The patient was comfortable at rest, breathing easily on room air, in no acute distress. Heart rhythm was regular and the lungs were clear.

**Results.** The 12-lead ECG showed no ischemic changes. The first <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL, drawn 19:28</ref>, and the repeat <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL, drawn 22:28</ref>, were both below the reporting threshold. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 19:28 showed sodium 136, potassium 4.3, chloride 100, bicarbonate 27, anion gap 9, urea nitrogen 16, creatinine 2.10 (H), eGFR 36 (L) and glucose 129 (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The pain was not accompanied by any change in vital signs that would suggest an unstable process, and the patient remained stable throughout the ED stay.
2. Elevated creatinine and reduced eGFR on the 19:28 panel, with normal electrolytes and a normal anion gap. This should be followed up with the primary care physician, and the patient was advised to arrange that visit in the coming days.
3. Blood pressure 151/91 at triage. Recheck with the primary care physician at follow-up.

The patient was given return precautions for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting or any new symptoms, and agreed with the plan.
