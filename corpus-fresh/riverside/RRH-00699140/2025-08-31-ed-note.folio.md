## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Weakness and poor oral intake, with abnormal labs.

**History.** The patient came to the ED today with several days of feeling run down and lightheaded, with very little to drink. They have a list of home medications that was not yet reconciled at the time of this note: metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, lisinopril 40 mg daily, hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily, and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly. **Allergies:** no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 13:11: BP 150/90, HR 110, RR 20, T 98.4 F, SpO2 97%. Tired-appearing but conversant. Tachycardic with a regular rhythm, dry mucous membranes, lungs clear, abdomen soft and nontender, no focal weakness.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 8/31 13:26: sodium 132 mmol/L (L), potassium 6.2 mmol/L (HH), chloride 98, bicarbonate 21 (L), anion gap 13, urea nitrogen 55 mg/dL (H), creatinine 1.99 mg/dL (H), eGFR 26 (L), glucose 203 mg/dL (H)</ref>.

**ED course.** The critical potassium was called to me. I gave calcium gluconate 1 g IV from 13:45 to 13:55 for membrane stabilization. Normal saline 1000 mL bolus was started at 13:55, then 125 mL/h, and is still running.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with volume depletion likely contributing. Continue IV fluids.
2. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, treated with calcium as above. Admit to the hospitalist service for monitoring and repeat labs.
