## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the ED after several days of diarrhea and poor intake, feeling weak and lightheaded on standing. No chest pain, no fainting, and no fever. Home medications are on the list and have not yet been reconciled. Allergy: lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** Vitals at 15:40: BP 136/82, HR 105, RR 19, T 98.2 F, SpO2 99%. Mildly tachycardic. Dry mucous membranes. Lungs clear, abdomen soft and nontender, no focal weakness.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 16:01 showed sodium 133 mmol/L, potassium 6.8 mmol/L (critical), chloride 98, bicarbonate 23, anion gap 12, urea nitrogen 35, creatinine 3.44, eGFR 18 and glucose 184.</ref> The potassium is markedly high and the creatinine is well above what would be expected for a patient who walks in feeling this well.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, the most urgent problem. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 16:14 to 16:24 to protect the heart while the potassium is being addressed. Cardiac monitoring continues.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, most consistent with volume depletion from the diarrhea and poor intake. Started 0.9% saline, a 1000 mL bolus at 16:24 and then 125 mL/h. Repeat labs after fluids.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for monitoring and further treatment. The patient and family were told the reason for admission and agree.
