## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with malaise, nausea and abdominal discomfort. They were breathing deeply on arrival but were not short of breath at rest. Home medications are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, losartan 100 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg (half of a 25 mg tablet) daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly. They also take <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, which has not yet been reconciled. Allergies: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:26: BP 116/70, HR 99, RR 16, T 98.4 F, SpO2 97%. Uncomfortable but alert. Mucous membranes are dry. Abdomen is soft with mild diffuse tenderness and no guarding.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 18:45 shows sodium 132, potassium 5.1, chloride 96, bicarbonate 10, anion gap 26, urea nitrogen 34, creatinine 1.03, eGFR 66 and glucose 220</ref>. The venous blood gas at 18:45 shows pH 7.12 and pCO2 27. Beta-hydroxybutyrate is 5.8 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a wide anion gap acidosis with a markedly elevated ketone level and a glucose of only 220. This pattern fits the SGLT2 inhibitor the patient takes at home. I started an insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h at 19:30, and it is running now.
2. Abdominal discomfort: <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> remains a working consideration while the work-up is completed.
3. The patient will be admitted to the hospitalist service for continued DKA management. The hospitalist has been called and the handoff given.
