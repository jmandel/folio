## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is a 59-year-old who came to the ED feeling very unwell, with several days of nausea, vomiting and poor appetite, and breathing that has become fast and deep. There is some abdominal discomfort. No known drug allergies. Home medications include <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, which has not yet been reconciled; the other home medications have also not been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:01: BP 99/59, HR 97, RR 22, temperature 98.7 F, SpO2 94%. She looks dry and uncomfortable, with deep, rapid respirations. The abdomen is soft.

**Results.** On the 21:28 panel: <ref id="bmp">sodium 130, potassium 5.1, chloride 94, bicarbonate 10, anion gap 26, urea nitrogen 27, creatinine 1.46, eGFR 62, glucose 191</ref>. Venous pH 7.19 with pCO2 24. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.1 mmol/L. The glucose is only modestly elevated despite the severe acidosis and large anion gap.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The acidosis, high anion gap and elevated ketones together establish the diagnosis, and the near-normal glucose fits her use of an SGLT2 inhibitor. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 22:05 and is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">Pancreatitis is being ruled out</ref> as a cause of her abdominal discomfort and vomiting; further testing is in progress.
3. Mild hyponatremia and a creatinine above normal, likely from volume depletion; follow on repeat panels.

I discussed the plan with the patient, who agrees with admission.
