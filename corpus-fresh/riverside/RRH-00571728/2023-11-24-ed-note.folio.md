## Emergency department provider note

**Chief complaint.** Swelling of the lips and tongue.

**History.** The patient presented this evening with swelling of the lips and a thick, heavy feeling in the tongue that developed over the past several hours. There was no hives, no itching, and no wheezing. The patient has not had an episode like this before and reports no new foods, no insect stings, and no new exposures. The patient takes <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily; stopped in the ED, with instructions not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Allergy list: no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:18: BP 111/67, HR 101, RR 19, temperature 98.4 F, SpO2 99%. Comfortable, speaking in full sentences, handling secretions. The upper lip is visibly swollen and the tongue is mildly enlarged. The voice is normal and there is no stridor. Lungs are clear with no wheeze. Skin shows no rash or urticaria.

**Course.** Because of the lip and tongue swelling, methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 23:37. The airway was watched closely and remained patent, with oxygen saturation staying at 99% on room air. There was no respiratory distress during the time I observed the patient.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The picture fits: swelling of the lips and tongue without hives or wheeze, in a patient on a daily ACE inhibitor. Lisinopril is stopped and the patient was told clearly that no ACE inhibitor should be taken again. Methylprednisolone given as above. Continue close airway monitoring and repeat the exam of the lips, tongue, and voice over the next hours. The patient and family were told to call for help immediately if breathing or swallowing becomes difficult or the swelling spreads. Disposition will depend on how the swelling evolves during observation.
