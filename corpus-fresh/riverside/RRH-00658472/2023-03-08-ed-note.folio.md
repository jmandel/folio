## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the ED on 3/8 with swelling of the lips and face that developed over the morning. There was no rash and no itching. They described a tight feeling in the face but no trouble swallowing and no difficulty breathing. This has not happened before. The allergy list on arrival read NKDA. Home medications include lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily, metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly; these have not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:26: BP 127/76, HR 94, RR 18, T 98.4 F, SpO2 99%. The patient was comfortable and speaking in full sentences. Lip and perioral swelling was present. The voice was normal and there was no stridor. Lungs were clear. There was no urticaria.

**Course.** Given the swelling and the absence of hives, the picture is typical of a bradykinin-mediated process rather than a histamine-mediated allergic reaction. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 14:45. The airway was watched closely and remained stable with normal oxygen saturation.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. Airway is currently patent and breathing is unlabored. The patient will continue to be observed in the department for any progression of swelling. <ref id="med_lisinopril">Lisinopril is stopped: do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again.</ref> The patient understood this instruction and repeated it back.
2. The remaining home medications will be addressed at reconciliation.

Return immediately for any worsening swelling of the tongue or throat, trouble swallowing, or shortness of breath.
