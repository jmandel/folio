## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented on the evening of 3/29 with chest pain that began earlier in the day. They described a pressure-like discomfort in the center of the chest, not clearly related to exertion, with no radiation. There was no fainting and no leg swelling. The allergy list shows lisinopril (angioedema). Home medications include metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, amlodipine 10 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly; these have not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:40: BP 163/98, HR 102, RR 19, T 98.1 F, SpO2 99%. The patient appeared comfortable and in no acute distress. Heart sounds were regular without a murmur. Lungs were clear. The chest wall was non-tender and the abdomen was soft.

**Results.** The ECG showed no ischemic changes. Troponin I was <0.01 ng/mL on the 21:01 draw (<ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01</ref>) and <0.01 ng/mL again on the 00:01 draw on 3/30 (<ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01</ref>). The basic metabolic panel at 21:01 showed sodium 138, potassium 4.4, chloride 101, bicarbonate 28, anion gap 9, urea nitrogen 20, creatinine 0.62, eGFR 93 and glucose 113 (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. Pain-free and comfortable by the end of the evaluation.
2. Blood pressure was elevated at triage, 163/98; this is noted for outpatient follow-up.
3. Renal function and electrolytes are normal.

The patient will follow up with their primary care physician within the week. Return for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting.
