## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department on the evening of 12/14 feeling unwell, with fatigue, thirst and rapid breathing. Glucose was not markedly elevated, which prompted a check for an acid-base problem. No known drug allergies. The home list includes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, which has not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:54: BP 118/71, HR 102, RR 21, T 98.7 F, SpO2 98%. The patient looks tired and is breathing deeply and somewhat quickly. Mucous membranes are dry. The abdomen is soft. No focal neurologic deficit.

**Results.** On the 00:23 panel the <ref id="bmp">sodium was 135 (L), potassium 4.7, chloride 100, bicarbonate 14 (L), anion gap 21 (H), urea nitrogen 32 (H), creatinine 1.61 (H), eGFR 34 (L) and glucose 157 (H)</ref>. The venous blood gas showed pH 7.10 (LL) with pCO2 26 (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate was 4.4 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis</ref>: a wide anion gap metabolic acidosis with a markedly elevated ketone level despite a near-normal glucose. Start an insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h, with dextrose-containing fluids because glucose is not high. Repeat chemistries and ketones every few hours, with potassium monitored closely during insulin therapy.
2. Acute kidney injury is likely given the creatinine of 1.61 and urea nitrogen of 32, probably from volume depletion. Fluids as above and avoid nephrotoxins.
3. Disposition: admit to the hospitalist service for ongoing DKA management and monitoring.
