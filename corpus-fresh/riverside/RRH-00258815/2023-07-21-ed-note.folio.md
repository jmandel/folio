## ED provider note

**History.** The patient is a 67-year-old man who came to the emergency department feeling generally unwell, with fatigue and low urine output over the past several days. He denies chest pain, fainting, or palpitations. He has no known drug allergies. His home medication list has not yet been reconciled; this will be completed once the acute problems are addressed.

**Exam.** Vitals at 17:47: BP 124/74, HR 107, RR 17, T 98.7 F, SpO2 98%. He is mildly tachycardic. Mucous membranes are somewhat dry. Lungs are clear, heart rhythm is regular, and the abdomen is soft and nontender. He is alert and conversant, with no focal weakness.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The basic metabolic panel drawn at 17:59 shows sodium 136, potassium 6.3 (critical), chloride 101, bicarbonate 21, anion gap 14, urea nitrogen 42, creatinine 1.93, eGFR 33, and glucose 150.</ref> The potassium was called to me as a critical value.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with urea nitrogen and creatinine well above normal and an eGFR of 33. He appears volume depleted. Normal saline 1000 mL bolus was started at 18:31, followed by 125 mL/h, and is still running. We will recheck his chemistry after fluids and admit him for further evaluation of the cause.
2. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.3. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 18:21 to 18:31 to protect the heart. He remains hemodynamically stable and will be monitored closely.
3. The hospitalist team has been called to admit him, and he is stable for transfer to the floor.
