## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the ED with several days of nausea, vomiting and poor oral intake, and now feels weak and tired with a dry mouth. There has been no chest pain. The patient takes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, which has not yet been reconciled in the ED. Other home medications are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, lisinopril 10 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly; none of these has been reconciled yet either. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 17:54: BP 102/61, HR 110, RR 17, temperature 98.2 F, SpO2 96%. The patient looks dehydrated, with dry mucous membranes and a fast heart rate. Lungs are clear. The abdomen is soft without guarding.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The basic metabolic panel drawn at 18:09 shows sodium 130, potassium 5.4, chloride 95, bicarbonate 11, anion gap 24, urea nitrogen 39, creatinine 1.07, eGFR 77 and glucose 151.</ref> The venous blood gas drawn at 18:09 shows pH 7.16 with pCO2 27. Beta-hydroxybutyrate is 4.3 mmol/L. The glucose is only mildly raised, yet the patient has a wide-gap metabolic acidosis with marked ketosis.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis</ref>. Anion gap acidosis with an elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate and a near-normal glucose, in a patient who takes an SGLT2 inhibitor and has been vomiting. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 18:58 and is running. The patient is being admitted to the hospitalist service for further management.
2. Hyponatremia and hyperkalemia on the panel above, in the setting of volume depletion. Both will be rechecked with the next chemistry panel.
3. The medication list above is still to be reconciled by the admitting team.
