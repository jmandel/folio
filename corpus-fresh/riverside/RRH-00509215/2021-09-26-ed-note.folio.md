## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented on the afternoon of 9/26 with swelling of the lips and tongue that had come on over the past several hours. They described a thick, full feeling in the mouth and some difficulty speaking clearly, but said they were able to swallow their saliva and had no wheezing. They take <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily, which is stopped; the instruction given is "Do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again"</ref>. Other home medications on the list are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily and amlodipine 5 mg daily; none of these has been reconciled yet. The allergy list reads NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 13:11: BP 121/73, HR 97, RR 23, T 99.1 F, SpO2 98%. The patient was alert and speaking in short phrases. There was visible swelling of the lips and the tongue was enlarged. The airway was patent at the time of examination, with no stridor. Lungs were clear. There was no rash and no hives on the skin.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling developed in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor, with no hives or wheezing to suggest an allergic cause. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 13:30. The airway is being watched closely given the tongue swelling and the respiratory rate of 23, and the patient was told to report any change in voice, trouble swallowing or trouble breathing right away. The ACE inhibitor is stopped, and the patient and family were told clearly that it must not be taken again, nor any other drug in that class.
2. Home metformin, glipizide and amlodipine are listed above and will be reconciled once the patient's course in the department is clear.

The patient and family understand the reason for close observation and agree with the plan.
