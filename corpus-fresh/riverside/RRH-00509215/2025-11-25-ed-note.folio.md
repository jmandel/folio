## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient came in on the evening of 11/25 feeling unwell, with nausea, abdominal discomfort, fatigue and a sense of breathing faster than usual. Home medications on the list are <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg by mouth daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, insulin glargine 10 units nightly and amlodipine 10 mg daily; none has been reconciled yet. Allergy: lisinopril (angioedema).

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:11: BP 104/62, HR 109, RR 17, T 98.1 F, SpO2 94%. The patient looked tired and dry, with a fast heart rate. The abdomen was soft with mild upper discomfort on palpation.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 19:26: sodium 131 (L), potassium 4.7, chloride 99, bicarbonate 9 (LL), anion gap 23 (H), urea nitrogen 38 (H), creatinine 0.98, eGFR 67, glucose 217 (H)</ref>. Venous blood gas at 19:26: pH 7.20 (L), pCO2 24 (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.4 mmol/L at 19:26.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a wide anion gap acidosis with a markedly low bicarbonate, an elevated ketone level and a glucose of only 217. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 20:15 and is still running. The SGLT2 inhibitor on the home list is the likely contributor to the picture, and the admitting team will address it.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given the abdominal discomfort and nausea; this remains a working consideration for now.
3. Hyponatremia, sodium 131, and a raised urea nitrogen, consistent with volume depletion. Creatinine is 0.98.

The patient is being admitted to the hospitalist service for continued treatment and monitoring.
