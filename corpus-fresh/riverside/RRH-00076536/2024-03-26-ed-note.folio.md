## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, weakness and shortness of breath.

**History.** The patient is a man with type 2 diabetes and hypertension who came in today feeling unwell, with nausea, marked fatigue and a sense of breathing hard even at rest. He reports poor food intake over the last few days. His home medication list includes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, along with metformin, semaglutide, lisinopril, hydrochlorothiazide, carvedilol, spironolactone, furosemide and atorvastatin. None of these have been reconciled yet in the ED. He has no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:26: BP 99/59, HR 105, RR 19, T 98.9 F, SpO2 98%. He appears dehydrated, with dry mucous membranes and deep, rapid respirations. Abdomen is soft, and he is alert and answering questions appropriately.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 16:53: sodium 132, potassium 4.7, chloride 96, bicarbonate 13, anion gap 23, urea nitrogen 39, creatinine 1.53, eGFR 49, glucose 232.</ref> Venous blood gas showed pH 7.15 with pCO2 25. Beta-hydroxybutyrate was 4.5 mmol/L. The glucose is only mildly elevated despite the severity of the acidosis.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, with a wide anion gap, low bicarbonate and elevated ketones. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h is ordered, with IV fluids, and he will be admitted to the hospitalist service for continued monitoring.
2. Acute volume depletion on exam and borderline blood pressure, with an elevated creatinine and urea nitrogen. Fluids as above.
3. Admission to the hospitalist team; care was handed off for the history and physical.
