## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening feeling unwell, with several days of weakness, nausea and poor appetite, and with deep, fast breathing on arrival. Home medications on the list are <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, lisinopril 40 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly. None of these has been reconciled yet in the ED. Allergies: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:11: BP 101/61, HR 85, RR 21, T 98.9 F, SpO2 94%. The patient looked tired and mildly dry, with rapid deep respirations. The abdomen was soft with mild diffuse discomfort and no guarding.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 21:25 shows sodium 130, potassium 4.6, chloride 96, bicarbonate 11, anion gap 23, urea nitrogen 35, creatinine 2.17 (eGFR 37) and glucose 206</ref>. Venous blood gas at 21:25: pH 7.17, pCO2 29. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.8 mmol/L from the same draw.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a wide anion gap acidosis with ketones markedly elevated while glucose is only modestly high. Insulin regular IV infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 22:15 and is still running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> remains a working consideration given the abdominal discomfort and nausea; lipase has been sent and the hospitalist will follow it up.
3. Acute kidney injury is suggested by the creatinine of 2.17 and urea nitrogen of 35; the hospital team will trend it with serial chemistries.

Care was handed off to the admitting hospitalist after the infusion was running.
