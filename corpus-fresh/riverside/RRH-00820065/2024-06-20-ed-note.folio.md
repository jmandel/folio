## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening with swelling of the lips and face that began earlier today. There was no hives and no itching. They described a thick feeling in the mouth but no trouble breathing and no trouble swallowing. They take <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily, which is stopped; the instruction is "Do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again"</ref>. They also take atorvastatin 20 mg nightly, which has not been reconciled in this visit. Allergy list: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:47: BP 148/89, HR 91, RR 18, T 98.9 F, SpO2 98%. The patient is sitting upright, speaking in full sentences, and is in no distress. There is visible swelling of the lips and the lower face. The voice is normal, there is no stridor, and the airway is patent. Lungs are clear to auscultation and the heart rhythm is regular.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The picture fits: swelling of the lips and face without hives or itching, in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor. The airway is currently stable, with normal oxygen saturation and no stridor. I gave methylprednisolone 125 mg IV once, started 20:06. We will keep the patient on monitoring and recheck the lips, tongue and voice repeatedly for any progression. If the swelling spreads toward the tongue or throat, we will call anesthesia early for airway control.
2. Lisinopril is stopped, and the patient and family were told that no ACE inhibitor should be taken again. This was explained in plain words, including that the swelling can return even after months of uneventful use of the drug.
3. Disposition will depend on the response over the next several hours of observation. The patient understands that any worsening of breathing, voice change or tongue swelling should be reported to the nurse immediately.

Discussed with the patient, who agrees with the plan.
