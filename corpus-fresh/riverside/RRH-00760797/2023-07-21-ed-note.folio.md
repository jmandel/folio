## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is a man with type 2 diabetes and hypertension who came to the ED feeling weak, nauseated and short of breath, with a poor appetite over the last few days. He describes deep, fast breathing and a dry mouth. He denies chest pain and fever. Allergies: NKDA.

His home medication list includes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>. The list also shows metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, losartan 50 mg daily, hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly. None of these has been reconciled yet.

**Exam.** Vitals at 22:54: BP 115/69, HR 105, RR 22, T 98.2 F, SpO2 97%. He looks dry and uncomfortable, with deep respirations. Mucous membranes are dry. Heart is tachycardic and regular, lungs are clear, and the abdomen is soft without focal tenderness.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:15 shows sodium 130, potassium 4.6, chloride 99, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 23, urea nitrogen 27, creatinine 1.44, eGFR 55 and glucose 161</ref>. Venous blood gas at 23:15: pH 7.19, pCO2 26. Beta-hydroxybutyrate is 7.2 mmol/L. The glucose is only mildly elevated despite a profound anion gap acidosis, which fits a euglycemic picture.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, in a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol (regular insulin, 0.1 units/kg/h) was started at 23:58 and is still running. He is being admitted to the hospitalist service, who will take over further management.
2. Mild hyponatremia and a creatinine of 1.44 with a low eGFR, in the setting of volume depletion. The hospitalist will follow these with repeat chemistries.
3. Home medications are still to be reconciled by the admitting team.
