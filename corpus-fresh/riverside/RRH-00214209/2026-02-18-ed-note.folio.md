## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with several days of nausea, vomiting and poor oral intake, and now feels weak and short of breath. No known drug allergies. Home medications include <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, along with metformin, dulaglutide, insulin glargine, lisinopril and amlodipine; none of the home medications has been reconciled yet in the ED.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:26: BP 117/70, HR 111, RR 22, T 98.8 F, SpO2 96%. The patient looks dry and uncomfortable, with deep, rapid respirations. Abdomen is soft with mild diffuse tenderness and no guarding.

**Results.** The venous blood gas drawn at 23:44 shows pH 7.11 and pCO2 21. Beta-hydroxybutyrate is 6.7 mmol/L. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 23:44: sodium 131, potassium 5.5, chloride 93, bicarbonate 11, anion gap 27, urea nitrogen 28, creatinine 2.39, eGFR 28, glucose 169.</ref> The glucose is only modestly elevated despite a profound anion gap acidosis.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, in a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor. Starting an insulin infusion per DKA protocol with dextrose-containing fluids because glucose is near normal. Repeat chemistries to follow the gap and potassium. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given the vomiting and abdominal tenderness; lipase is pending and will be followed.
3. Acute kidney injury with hyperkalemia, likely from volume depletion. Potassium will be rechecked as insulin and fluids are given, and the infusion will be adjusted to it.

The patient and family were told the diagnosis and the reason for admission, and agree with the plan.
