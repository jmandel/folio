## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is an adult with type 2 diabetes who came to the ED with several days of feeling unwell: nausea, fatigue and deep, fast breathing that became more noticeable today. There was no chest pain and no fever. On review of the home medication list, which has not yet been reconciled, the patient takes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg by mouth daily</ref>. Other home medications on the list are metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, dulaglutide 0.5 mg subcutaneously weekly, insulin glargine 10 units subcutaneously nightly, lisinopril 20 mg daily and hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily. Allergies: no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:26: BP 110/66, HR 83, RR 17, temperature 98.4 F, SpO2 95%. The patient looked tired and mildly dry, with deep unlabored respirations. The abdomen was soft with mild diffuse discomfort and no guarding.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 18:41 showed sodium 133 (L), potassium 4.6, chloride 98, bicarbonate 9 (LL), anion gap 26 (H), urea nitrogen 28 (H), creatinine 1.95 (H), eGFR 28 (L) and glucose 151 (H)</ref>. Venous blood gas at 18:41: pH 7.18 (LL), pCO2 20 (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.8 mmol/L. The glucose is only modestly raised despite a marked anion gap acidosis.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a wide anion gap, a low bicarbonate, an acidotic venous pH and a markedly raised ketone level with a near-normal glucose. The patient is being admitted to the hospitalist service for continued treatment of this.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> because of the abdominal discomfort and the ketoacidosis; this remains a working question while further labs return.
3. Kidney function is worse than expected, with creatinine 1.95 and eGFR 28, which will need to be followed.

The hospitalist has been called and agrees to accept the patient.
