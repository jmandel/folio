## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Abnormal labs and decreased urine output after several days of back pain.

**History.** The patient presented with several days of poor appetite and low fluid intake, and reports using over-the-counter pain medicine for back pain. No chest pain, no palpitations, no fainting. Allergies: NKDA. Home medications on the list are metformin 1000 mg twice daily, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, losartan 100 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily and amlodipine 5 mg daily; none has been reconciled in the ED yet.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:40: BP 116/70, HR 82, RR 20, T 98.7 F, SpO2 97%. Alert and conversant, appears mildly dry. Heart regular, lungs clear, abdomen soft and nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 14:49: sodium 131 (L), potassium 6.3 (HH), chloride 103, bicarbonate 17 (L), anion gap 11, urea nitrogen 54 (H), creatinine 3.28 (H), eGFR 19 (L), glucose 163 (H)</ref>.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.3. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 15:14 to 15:24 for membrane stabilization.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, creatinine 3.28 with urea nitrogen 54. Sodium chloride 0.9% 1000 mL bolus was started at 15:24, followed by 125 mL/h, and is still running.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for further treatment and repeat labs. The admitting physician will address the home medications.
