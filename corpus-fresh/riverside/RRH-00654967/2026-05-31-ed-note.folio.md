## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the emergency department on 5/31 with chest pain that brought them in this evening. The pain was not exertional in character, and there was no fainting. They were seen and examined promptly on arrival. They have no known drug allergies. Home medications on the list are metformin 500 mg twice daily, dapagliflozin 10 mg daily, glipizide 5 mg daily, insulin glargine 10 units nightly, and losartan 50 mg daily; none of these have been reconciled in the ED yet.

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:54: BP 165/99, HR 87, RR 17, temperature 98.1 F, SpO2 97%. The patient was comfortable at rest, speaking in full sentences, and in no acute distress. Heart rhythm was regular. Lungs were clear. The chest wall and abdomen were not tender to palpation.

**Results.** The 12-lead ECG showed no ischemic changes. <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on the 20:08 draw, and <ref id="trop_2">troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on the 23:08 repeat. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 20:08 showed sodium 138, potassium 4.3, chloride 104, bicarbonate 24, anion gap 10, urea nitrogen 19, creatinine 2.36 (H), eGFR 30 (L), and glucose 148 (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The patient remained pain-free and stable through the observation period in the department.
2. Blood pressure was elevated on arrival at 165/99, which I am noting for the patient's outpatient physician to follow.
3. Creatinine was 2.36 with an eGFR of 30 on today's panel. I have asked that the result be shared with the patient's primary care physician so that it can be compared with prior values.

The results and the reasoning for the work-up were explained to the patient, who understood. They should follow up with their primary care physician within the week and return to the emergency department for any recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting.
