## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with several days of nausea and poor appetite and now feels weak and short of breath. Per the medication history, the home list includes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, along with metformin, glipizide, semaglutide, losartan, hydrochlorothiazide, amlodipine and atorvastatin. No known drug allergies. Home medications have not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:01: BP 105/63, HR 101, RR 17, T 98.2 F, SpO2 97%. The patient looks tired and mildly dry. Breathing is deep but unlabored. The abdomen is soft with mild diffuse discomfort and no guarding.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 21:14 shows sodium 133 (L), potassium 5.5 (H), chloride 99, bicarbonate 8 (LL), anion gap 26 (H), urea nitrogen 29 (H), creatinine 1.17, eGFR 70, glucose 216 (H)</ref>. Venous blood gas at 21:14: pH 7.20 (L), pCO2 22 (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.2 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a wide anion gap acidosis with elevated ketones despite a glucose of only 216. In a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor this fits the picture. Insulin regular IV infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 22:05 and is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given the abdominal discomfort; this remains a working consideration while the workup is completed.
3. Potassium is elevated at 5.5 and will be rechecked as the acidosis is treated.

The hospitalist has been called and agrees with admission.
