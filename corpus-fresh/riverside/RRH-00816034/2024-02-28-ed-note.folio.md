## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the ED on the evening of 2/28 feeling weak and unwell, with nausea and a poor appetite over the preceding days. There was no chest pain. The patient takes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> at home, and also has metformin 1000 mg twice daily and losartan 100 mg daily on the home medication list. The allergy list reads NKDA.

**Exam.** At 20:26: BP 102/61, HR 105, RR 23, T 98.9 F, SpO2 97%. The patient appeared dry and was breathing somewhat deeply and quickly. The abdomen was soft. The patient was alert and answered questions appropriately.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 20:54 showed sodium 135, potassium 5.0, chloride 93, bicarbonate 14, anion gap 28, urea nitrogen 32, creatinine 1.39, eGFR 42 and glucose 151</ref>. Venous blood gas on the same draw: pH 7.13, pCO2 22. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.6 mmol/L. The glucose is near normal despite a marked anion gap acidosis and a high ketone level.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The picture of a wide anion gap, low bicarbonate, a venous pH of 7.13 and an elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate with a glucose of only 151 fits this diagnosis. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 21:30 and is still running.
2. Renal function: creatinine 1.39 with eGFR 42 and urea nitrogen 32, in keeping with volume depletion on top of the patient's baseline. Potassium is 5.0 and will be followed closely while insulin is running.

The patient is being admitted to the hospitalist service for continued treatment and monitoring. The patient and family were updated on the diagnosis and the plan and had no further questions at the time of this note.
