## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening with swelling of the lips and face that came on over the past several hours. They describe the lips as tight and puffy and feel that the tongue is somewhat thick, but they have been able to swallow their saliva and speak in full sentences. There is no rash and no itching. They take <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily, which is now stopped; the instruction given is not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Allergy list on arrival: NKDA. Atorvastatin 20 mg nightly is also on the home list and has not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:26: BP 114/68, HR 98, RR 16, temperature 98.4 F, SpO2 95%. The patient is anxious but comfortable sitting upright and speaking clearly, with no stridor. There is visible swelling of the lips and mild swelling of the lower face. The skin is clear without hives. Breathing is unlabored, and the lungs are clear to auscultation.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling is isolated to the lips and face, without hives, in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor, which fits this picture rather than an allergic reaction. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 19:45. The airway is currently protected and speech is normal, but this can progress, so the patient will stay on monitoring with SpO2 watched closely and the airway reassessed repeatedly. Any change in voice, drooling, trouble swallowing or noisy breathing should prompt immediate airway evaluation.
2. Lisinopril is stopped, and the patient and family were told clearly that no ACE inhibitor should be taken again. This was explained in plain words and they understood.

Disposition will depend on how the swelling behaves over the next period of observation.
