## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Chest pain.

**History.** The patient came to the ED on the evening of 1/20 with chest pain. The pain was not associated with exertion in any way that the patient could describe, and it did not worsen when they were lying flat. No fainting. They were comfortable and talking in full sentences when I first saw them.

**Allergies.** No known drug allergies.

**Home medications.** The home list was reviewed and has not yet been reconciled: metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, dapagliflozin 10 mg daily, lisinopril 40 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg (half of a 25 mg tablet) daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily, and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:18: BP 149/89, HR 94, RR 21, T 98.1 F, SpO2 98%. Alert and in no acute distress. Heart regular without murmur. Lungs clear. Chest wall and abdomen without focal tenderness. No leg swelling.

**Results.** ECG without ischemic changes. <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL on the 23:41 draw</ref> and <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL on the repeat at 02:41</ref>. Basic metabolic panel drawn 23:41: sodium 141, potassium 4.0, chloride 100, bicarbonate 23, anion gap 18 (H), urea nitrogen 17, creatinine 1.94 (H), eGFR 39 (L), glucose 161 (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The pain settled during the ED stay and the patient remained stable on monitoring.
2. Abnormal chemistry: creatinine 1.94 with eGFR 39 and an anion gap of 18, as above. These results are flagged for the primary care physician to review at follow-up, with a repeat panel.
3. Home medications are not yet reconciled and need review with the patient and their primary care physician.

The results were discussed with the patient, who was reassured. Return precautions were given for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting. Follow-up with primary care within the week.
