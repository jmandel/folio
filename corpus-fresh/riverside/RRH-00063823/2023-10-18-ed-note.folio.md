## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, fatigue and fast breathing.

**History.** The patient is an adult with diabetes who came in feeling weak and sick to his stomach, breathing fast, after several days of poor appetite and little fluid intake. He takes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, which has not yet been reconciled with the pharmacy. Other home medications (metformin, insulin glargine, atorvastatin) are also pending reconciliation. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** At 15:11: BP 118/71, HR 86, RR 20, T 98.2 F, SpO2 96%. He appears fatigued and dry, with deep respirations. The abdomen is soft with mild upper discomfort but no guarding.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The 15:28 basic metabolic panel shows sodium 134, potassium 4.8, chloride 93, bicarbonate 9, anion gap 32, urea nitrogen 32, creatinine 2.14, eGFR 35 and glucose 162.</ref> Venous gas at 15:28: pH 7.20, pCO2 25. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.5. Lipase 53.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a wide anion gap acidosis with elevated ketones despite a near-normal glucose. Start an insulin infusion per DKA protocol with fluids and dextrose, and follow chemistries closely. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given the upper abdominal discomfort; lipase is 53 and the abdominal exam is reassuring.
3. Kidney function is reduced with creatinine 2.14; this is probably from volume depletion and will be rechecked.
