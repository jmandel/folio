## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is a man with type 2 diabetes who came to the ED feeling unwell, with nausea, abdominal discomfort, fatigue and fast breathing over the past day or so. He takes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg by mouth daily</ref> at home; the home medication list has not yet been reconciled. He reports no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:11: BP 104/62, HR 82, RR 17, temperature 98.7 F, SpO2 94%. He appears tired and mildly dehydrated, with dry mucous membranes. Breathing is deep but unlabored. The abdomen is soft without guarding.

**Results.** The chemistry panel returned with a low bicarbonate and a wide gap despite only a moderately elevated glucose. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 21:34: sodium 128 mmol/L (L), potassium 5.3 mmol/L (H), chloride 100 mmol/L, bicarbonate 12 mmol/L (L), anion gap 16 mmol/L, urea nitrogen 29 mg/dL (H), creatinine 1.39 mg/dL (H), eGFR 47 mL/min/1.73m2 (L), glucose 229 mg/dL (H).</ref> The venous blood gas drawn at 21:34 showed pH 7.21 (L) and pCO2 25 mmHg (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate is 7.0 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: acidosis with a markedly elevated ketone level and a glucose well under the range usually seen in DKA. This fits the pattern seen with the SGLT2 inhibitor he takes. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h is being started, with dextrose-containing fluids so that glucose does not fall too low while the ketosis clears. Metabolic panel and ketones will be rechecked serially.
2. Hyponatremia and mild hyperkalemia, likely from volume depletion and acidosis; expect improvement with fluids and insulin. Creatinine is above what would be expected and will be followed.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for ongoing DKA management. The patient understands the plan and agrees with admission.
