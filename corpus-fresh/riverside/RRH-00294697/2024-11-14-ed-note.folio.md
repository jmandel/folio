## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with malaise, nausea and a feeling of being short of breath, with deep, fast breathing noted at triage. There is no known allergy to any drug (NKDA). The home medication list includes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, which has not yet been reconciled. Other home medications on the list, also not yet reconciled, are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, lisinopril 10 mg daily, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly.

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:26: BP 108/65, HR 104, RR 17, T 98.9 F, SpO2 94%. The patient looks tired and mildly dry. Respirations are deep. The abdomen is soft.

**Results.** On the 19:52 panel the <ref id="bmp">sodium was 135, potassium 4.8, chloride 99, bicarbonate 12, anion gap 24, urea nitrogen 35, creatinine 1.29, eGFR 54 and glucose 178</ref>. Venous blood gas at 19:52 showed pH 7.12 and pCO2 26. Beta-hydroxybutyrate was 5.2 mmol/L. The glucose is only modestly raised despite a marked acidosis with a wide gap.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The low pH and bicarbonate, wide anion gap and elevated ketones establish the diagnosis even though glucose is under 200. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 20:30 and is still running. Repeat chemistry will follow on the protocol schedule.
2. The patient is to be admitted to the hospitalist service for continued treatment and monitoring. The admitting physician has been called with the results.
