## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came in with swelling of the lips and tongue that developed over the last several hours, with a thick feeling in the mouth and some difficulty swallowing. There was no hives and no wheeze. The home list includes <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 10 mg daily, now stopped; the patient is not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Other home medications, not yet reconciled, are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly. The allergy list shows dapagliflozin (diabetic ketoacidosis).

**Exam.** Vitals at 13:11: BP 148/89, HR 92, RR 20, T 98.1 F, SpO2 94%. There is visible swelling of the lips and tongue. Speech is slightly muffled, but the patient is handling secretions and is able to speak in full sentences. Lungs are clear, and there is no stridor.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling is limited to the lips and tongue and there is no rash, which fits a bradykinin-mediated process rather than an allergic one. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 13:30. The airway is being watched closely, and the patient will be observed in the department with the head of the bed raised.
2. The patient and family were told about the reason for stopping lisinopril and why this class of medicine must be avoided in future.
3. Disposition will depend on how the swelling behaves over the next several hours.
