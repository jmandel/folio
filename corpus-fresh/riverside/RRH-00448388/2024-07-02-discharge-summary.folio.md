## Discharge summary

**Diagnosis:** <ref id="dx_dka">Euglycemic DKA, SGLT2 inhibitor-associated</ref>, resolved.

**Hospital course.** The patient was admitted with ketoacidosis despite a glucose of only 210. <ref id="precipitant">The ketoacidosis was precipitated by prolonged fasting for religious observance while continuing the SGLT2 inhibitor.</ref> The patient was treated with an insulin infusion and fluids, and the acidosis cleared. At discharge the patient is eating and feeling well. Vitals: BP 131/79, HR 88, RR 21, T 98.9 F, SpO2 95%.

**Results at discharge.** Basic metabolic panel at 06:18 on 7/2: sodium 132 mmol/L (L), potassium 4.3 mmol/L, chloride 99 mmol/L, bicarbonate 19 mmol/L (L), anion gap 14 mmol/L, urea nitrogen 22 mg/dL, creatinine 1.25 mg/dL (H), eGFR 53 mL/min/1.73m2 (L), glucose 158 mg/dL (H).

**Allergies:** no known drug allergies.

**Discharge medications**

- <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">Dapagliflozin 10 mg daily: do not resume until reviewed by the primary care physician</ref>
- <ref id="med_metformin">Metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals: continue</ref>
- <ref id="med_lisinopril">Lisinopril 40 mg daily: continue</ref>
- <ref id="med_chlorthalidone">Chlorthalidone 12.5 mg (half of a 25 mg tablet) daily: continue</ref>

**Follow-up.** See primary care at Northgate Family Medicine within 1 week. At that visit the physician will decide whether to restart dapagliflozin, and the basic metabolic panel should be rechecked. The patient was told to return for vomiting, abdominal pain, fast breathing or marked weakness.
