## ED provider note

**History.** The patient was brought in this evening feeling unwell, with nausea and marked fatigue, and was found to be acidotic on testing. Home medications are listed below and have not yet been reconciled. They include <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, lisinopril 40 mg daily, and chlorthalidone 12.5 mg (half of a 25 mg tablet) daily. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:26: BP 99/59, HR 92, RR 18, T 99.1 F, SpO2 98%. The patient appears tired and mildly dry, speaks in full sentences, and is comfortable at rest. The abdomen is soft.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:49 shows sodium 129 mmol/L (L), potassium 5.0 mmol/L, chloride 95 mmol/L (L), bicarbonate 13 mmol/L (L), anion gap 21 mmol/L (H), urea nitrogen 27 mg/dL (H), creatinine 1.48 mg/dL (H), eGFR 43 mL/min/1.73m2 (L), and glucose 210 mg/dL (H)</ref>. The venous blood gas drawn at the same time shows pH 7.17 and pCO2 24 mmHg. Beta-hydroxybutyrate is 6.2 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The glucose is only modestly elevated, but the low bicarbonate, wide anion gap, venous pH of 7.17 and beta-hydroxybutyrate of 6.2 together establish the diagnosis. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 00:30 and is running. The patient will be admitted to the hospitalist service.
2. Hyponatremia, sodium 129, and creatinine above normal at 1.48; both will be followed on repeat panels.
3. Medication reconciliation is pending and will be completed by the admitting team.
