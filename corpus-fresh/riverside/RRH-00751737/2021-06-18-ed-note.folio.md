## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient came to the ED with chest pain and was seen at 15:48. The discomfort was not reproduced by movement, and there was no fainting. Allergies: no known drug allergies. Home medications on the list are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, lisinopril 10 mg daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly; these have not been reconciled during this visit.

**Exam.** Vitals at 15:54: BP 159/95, HR 80, RR 21, temperature 98.2 F, SpO2 99%. The patient was comfortable, speaking in full sentences, and in no distress. Heart rhythm was regular, lungs were clear, and the chest wall and abdomen were soft.

**Results.** The ECG showed no ischemic changes. <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on the 16:20 panel and <ref id="trop_2">troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on the repeat at 19:20. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 16:20 showed sodium 139, potassium 4.6, chloride 105, bicarbonate 23, anion gap 11, urea nitrogen 20, creatinine 1.42 (H), eGFR 59 (L) and glucose 132 (H). The anion gap is normal, and the creatinine and eGFR are mildly abnormal as reported by the lab.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. Vital signs were stable throughout the visit and the pain did not recur while in the department. The patient was told the results were reassuring.
2. Blood pressure was 159/95 on arrival, and I have asked that it be rechecked by the primary care physician.
3. Creatinine 1.42 with eGFR 59 on this panel; I recommend repeating this with the primary care physician at follow-up.

Return precautions were reviewed: return for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or any new concern. Follow-up with the primary care physician within the next week.
