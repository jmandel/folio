## Emergency department provider note

**Chief complaint.** Swelling of the lips and face.

**History.** The patient came to the ED on the evening of 6/16 because of swelling of the lips and face that had come on over the past several hours. There was no hives and no itching. They described the swelling as tight and uncomfortable but did not have trouble breathing or swallowing, and no one else at home was ill. They take lisinopril 10 mg by mouth daily for blood pressure. Allergy list: NKDA.

Home medications on the list, not yet reconciled at the time of this note, are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:11: BP 115/69, HR 91, RR 17, temperature 98.8 F, SpO2 96%. The patient was sitting upright, speaking in full sentences, and not in distress. There was visible swelling of the lips and lower face. No stridor and no drooling. Lungs were clear. Skin showed no rash.

**Course in the ED.** Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 23:30. The airway was watched closely and remained patent, and the patient stayed comfortable and hemodynamically stable on repeat checks.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling without hives or itching, in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor, fits this picture rather than an allergic reaction. The patient was told: <ref id="med_lisinopril">do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Continue airway monitoring and reassess the swelling after the steroid dose.
2. Medication reconciliation of the remaining home medications to be completed with the admitting team.

The patient understands the reason for monitoring and agrees with the plan.
