## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented on the evening of 6/14 after several days of feeling unwell, with weakness and poor intake. Allergies: NKDA. The home medication list has not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 22:47: BP 128/77, HR 91, RR 20, T 99 F, SpO2 94% on room air. The patient is tired-appearing but conversant and oriented. Mucous membranes are dry. Heart rate is regular, lungs are clear, and the abdomen is soft and nontender.

**Results.** The basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:09 shows <ref id="bmp">sodium 136, potassium 6.3 (critical), chloride 100, bicarbonate 17, anion gap 19, urea nitrogen 51, creatinine 4.02, eGFR 16, glucose 185</ref>.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.3. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 23:21 to 23:31 for membrane stabilization. The patient is being monitored on telemetry.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, creatinine 4.02 with a low eGFR and an elevated anion gap with low bicarbonate. Started 0.9% saline, a 1000 mL bolus at 23:31, then 125 mL/h, still running. We will repeat the chemistry panel after the fluids.
3. The patient will be admitted to the hospitalist service for further management of the kidney injury and potassium. Hospitalist Dr. Castellano has been called.
4. Home medications have not been reconciled in the ED and will be addressed by the admitting team.
