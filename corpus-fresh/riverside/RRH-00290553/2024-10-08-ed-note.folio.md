## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Weakness and decreased urination.

**History.** The patient presented this afternoon feeling run down, with several days of poor oral intake and less urine than usual. No vomiting, no chest pain, no fever at home. Home medications on arrival were metformin 500 mg twice daily, dapagliflozin 10 mg daily, lisinopril 20 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly; none have been reconciled yet. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** At 17:11: BP 119/71, HR 103, RR 24, T 99.2 F, SpO2 94%. Appears tired but alert and conversant. Mucous membranes dry. Lungs clear. Abdomen soft, nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 17:20: sodium 129 (L), potassium 6.4 (critical), chloride 99, bicarbonate 19 (L), anion gap 11, urea nitrogen 47, creatinine 3.19, eGFR 15, glucose 183.</ref>

**ED course.** Given the critical potassium, calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 17:45 to 17:55. Normal saline 1000 mL IV bolus was started at 17:55, then 125 mL/h, and is still running.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with creatinine 3.19 and urea nitrogen 47. Continue IV fluids.
2. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.4, treated with IV calcium as above. Hospitalist consulted for admission.
