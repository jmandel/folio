## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department and was evaluated at 23:48. Initial vitals at 23:54 were BP 142/85, HR 110, RR 20, T 99 F, SpO2 95%. The allergy list reads NKDA. The home medication list (empagliflozin 10 mg daily, losartan 50 mg daily, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily, levothyroxine 75 mcg daily) has not yet been reconciled, so I am not making decisions about the individual home medications in the ED.

**Exam.** The patient is tachycardic and mildly tachypneic but in no acute distress. Mucous membranes appear somewhat dry. Lungs are clear, and the abdomen is soft and nontender.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 00:03 showed sodium 133 (L), potassium 6.8 (HH), chloride 101, bicarbonate 19 (L), anion gap 13, urea nitrogen 48 (H), creatinine 2.19 (H), eGFR 22 (L) and glucose 116 (H)</ref>. The potassium is critically high and the renal function is markedly reduced.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.8. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 00:28 to 00:38 for membrane protection. Repeat potassium to be checked after treatment.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with creatinine 2.19 and urea nitrogen 48. Normal saline 1000 mL IV bolus was started at 00:38, followed by 125 mL/h, and it is still running. Monitor urine output and recheck the BMP.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for continued treatment and monitoring. Home medications are to be reconciled on admission.

Handoff given to Dr. Castellano.
