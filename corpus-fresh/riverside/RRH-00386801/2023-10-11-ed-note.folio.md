## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is an adult with type 2 diabetes who came to the ED feeling unwell, with nausea, malaise and fatigue over the past day. There was no chest pain. Home medications include <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg by mouth daily</ref>, metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly; the medication list has not yet been reconciled. Allergy list: no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:26: BP 108/65, HR 86, RR 17, temperature 99.1 F, SpO2 94%. Appears mildly dehydrated and tired but is conversant and in no acute distress. Respirations are unlabored.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 23:37: sodium 133 (L), potassium 5.2 (H), chloride 100, bicarbonate 14 (L), anion gap 19 (H), urea nitrogen 39 (H), creatinine 0.88, eGFR 73, glucose 208 (H).</ref> Venous blood gas drawn 23:37 shows pH 7.20 (L) with pCO2 30 (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate is 5.4 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. Glucose is only mildly elevated, but the low bicarbonate, wide anion gap, acidic venous pH and elevated ketones make the diagnosis clear. The patient takes an SGLT2 inhibitor, which fits this picture. Insulin regular infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h, started 00:30, with dextrose 5% in lactated Ringer's as glucose is near normal. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> as a working diagnosis while the evaluation of the cause of the acidosis is completed; a lipase was sent with the 23:37 draw.
3. Hyperkalemia is mild in the setting of acidosis and is expected to shift as insulin is given; repeat chemistry will follow.

The patient and family were told the plan and agree with admission.
