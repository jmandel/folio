## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department on the evening of 8/27 feeling unwell, with nausea, marked fatigue and fast, deep breathing. Their home medications are listed below and have not yet been reconciled with the pharmacy. They take <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg by mouth daily</ref>, metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, losartan 100 mg daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly. Allergy list: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:26: BP 102/61, HR 88, RR 22, T 99.1 F, SpO2 97%. The patient looks tired and mildly dry, with deep, somewhat rapid respirations. Abdomen is soft. Mental status is normal and they answer questions appropriately.

**Results.** The basic metabolic panel drawn at 21:51 shows <ref id="bmp">sodium 132, potassium 5.6, chloride 96, bicarbonate 14, anion gap 22, urea nitrogen 40, creatinine 2.42, eGFR 30 and glucose 196</ref>. Venous blood gas drawn at the same time: pH 7.15, pCO2 25. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.6 mmol/L. The glucose is only modestly elevated, but the low bicarbonate, wide anion gap, acidic pH and high ketone level together make this a ketoacidosis rather than simple dehydration.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. An insulin regular infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 22:30 and is running. The patient has been taking an SGLT2 inhibitor, which fits this picture. Serial chemistries are planned while the infusion continues, with close attention to the potassium of 5.6 and to the creatinine of 2.42, which is elevated.
2. Disposition: admit to the hospitalist service for continued treatment and monitoring. I discussed the findings and the plan with the patient, who agrees.
