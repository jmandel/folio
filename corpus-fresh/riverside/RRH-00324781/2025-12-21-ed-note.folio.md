## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Diarrhea for several days, weakness, and abnormal labs.

**History.** The patient came to the ED feeling weak and lightheaded after several days of loose stools and little oral intake. There was no chest pain and no fever. Home medications are lisinopril 20 mg daily, metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly; reconciliation has not been completed. The allergy list reads NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:18: BP 115/69, HR 112, RR 18, temperature 98.3 F, SpO2 94%. The patient looks tired and mildly dehydrated, with dry mucous membranes and a fast but regular pulse. Lungs are clear. Abdomen is soft and nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel: sodium 136, potassium 6.5 (critical), chloride 98, bicarbonate 20, anion gap 18, urea nitrogen 43, creatinine 1.70, eGFR 32, glucose 135.</ref>

**ED course.** Given the potassium, IV calcium gluconate 1 g was given at 16:52 and finished at 17:02. Normal saline 1000 mL bolus was started at 17:02, to continue at 125 mL/h.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.5, treated with calcium as above.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with creatinine 1.70 and a high urea nitrogen, in the setting of volume loss from diarrhea. Continue IV fluids.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for further treatment and monitoring. Care was handed off to Dr. Whitaker.
