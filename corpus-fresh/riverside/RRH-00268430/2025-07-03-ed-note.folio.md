## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came in with several days of diarrhea and poor oral intake, feeling weak and tired, with little urine over the past day. No chest pain. Allergy list: lisinopril (cough). Home medicines were listed but not yet reconciled at the time of this note.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:01: BP 136/82, HR 98, RR 21, T 98.7 F, SpO2 94%. Appears tired but is alert and conversant. Dry mucous membranes. Lungs clear, abdomen soft and nontender.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 14:15 showed sodium 132 (L), potassium 6.5 (HH), chloride 102, bicarbonate 19 (L), anion gap 11, urea nitrogen 54 (H), creatinine 2.47 (H), eGFR 22 (L), and glucose 112 (H)</ref>.

**Course and assessment.** The panel shows <ref id="dx_aki">acute kidney injury</ref> together with <ref id="dx_hyperk">hyperkalemia</ref> at a critical potassium of 6.5. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 14:35 to 14:45 for membrane stabilization. A 1000 mL normal saline bolus was started at 14:45, to be followed by 125 mL/h, and it is still running. The patient tolerated both without difficulty. Hospital admission is requested for ongoing fluids, repeat potassium and creatinine, and monitoring. The hospitalist team has been called and will see the patient on the floor.
