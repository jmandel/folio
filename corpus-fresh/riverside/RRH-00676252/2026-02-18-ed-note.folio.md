## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient came to the ED feeling unwell, with nausea, fatigue and fast, deep breathing. There was no chest pain and no fever. Allergy list: no known drug allergies.

**Home medications.** <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">Dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> is on the home list and has not yet been reconciled. Also listed and not yet reconciled: metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, lisinopril 10 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly.

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:01: BP 116/70, HR 83, RR 23, temperature 98.2 F, SpO2 97%. The patient is alert and conversant but breathing deeply and quickly. Mucous membranes are somewhat dry. Abdomen is soft and non-tender. Lungs are clear.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn at 20:18: pH 7.11, pCO2 28 mmHg. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.0 mmol/L, drawn at 20:18. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 20:18: sodium 129, potassium 4.7, chloride 96, bicarbonate 13, anion gap 20, urea nitrogen 31, creatinine 1.57, eGFR 40, glucose 230.</ref>

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The picture is a wide anion gap metabolic acidosis with markedly elevated ketones despite a glucose of only 230, in a patient who takes an SGLT2 inhibitor. The acidosis is significant, so the patient will be admitted and managed under the DKA protocol, with an insulin infusion and repeat chemistries.
2. Hyponatremia and reduced kidney function, with creatinine 1.57 and urea nitrogen 31, consistent with volume depletion. These will be rechecked with the repeat panels.
3. The hospitalist has been called for admission, and the patient understands the plan.
