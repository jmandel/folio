## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department this evening with a cough and feeling unwell. On arrival the cough was productive, and the patient described feeling run down and short of breath with exertion. Home medications as listed in the chart are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, dapagliflozin 10 mg daily, losartan 100 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg (half of a 25 mg tablet) daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly. These have not yet been reconciled. Lisinopril is listed as an allergy (cough).

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:01: BP 129/77, HR 104, RR 18, T 98.8 F, SpO2 96%. The patient appeared mildly fatigued but was speaking in full sentences. Breath sounds were diminished at the base with a few crackles. Heart rhythm was regular, tachycardic. Abdomen soft and nontender. No leg swelling.

**Results.** The <ref id="cbc">CBC drawn at 20:17 showed white blood cells 12.2 (H), hemoglobin 13.7 and platelets 316</ref>. Basic metabolic panel from the same draw: sodium 138, potassium 4.6, chloride 102, bicarbonate 22, anion gap 14, urea nitrogen 39 (H), creatinine 1.57 (H), eGFR 41 (L), glucose 193 (H). The kidney numbers are elevated and I will ask the admitting team to follow them.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>, based on the cough, the exam findings and the elevated white count. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 21:25 and is still running.
2. Elevated urea nitrogen and creatinine with an eGFR of 41. Normal saline at 75 mL/h was started at 20:45 and is still running. Recheck the panel with morning labs.
3. Home medications are not yet reconciled; the admitting hospitalist will address them.

The patient is stable in the department and is being admitted to the hospitalist service.
