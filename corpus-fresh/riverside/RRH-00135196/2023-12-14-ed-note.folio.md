## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Diarrhea, weakness and lightheadedness.

**History.** The patient presents with four days of watery diarrhea and poor oral intake. They feel weak and dizzy when standing and have passed little urine today. No fever, no blood in the stool, no chest pain. Home medications were not yet reconciled at the time of this note: metformin, empagliflozin, lisinopril and rosuvastatin were reported. Allergies: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 13:47: BP 113/68, HR 100, RR 16, T 98.2 F, SpO2 97%. Appears dehydrated with dry mucous membranes. Heart rhythm regular, lungs clear, abdomen soft and non-tender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 14:03: sodium 135, potassium 6.4, chloride 97, bicarbonate 20, anion gap 18, urea nitrogen 34, creatinine 2.48, eGFR 22, glucose 168.</ref> The potassium was called as a critical value.

**ED course and assessment.** Findings are consistent with <ref id="dx_aki">acute kidney injury</ref> from volume depletion, complicated by <ref id="dx_hyperk">hyperkalemia</ref>. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 14:21 to 14:31 for membrane stabilization. A normal saline bolus of 1000 mL was started at 14:31, then 125 mL/h, and is still running. The patient is stable and being admitted to the hospitalist service for further fluid resuscitation, monitoring of potassium and renal function, and medication review.
