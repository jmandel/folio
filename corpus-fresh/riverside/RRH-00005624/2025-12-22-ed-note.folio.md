## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Diarrhea, weakness and poor oral intake.

**History.** The patient came in after several days of loose stools with little eating and drinking, and described feeling weak and lightheaded. No chest pain, no fever at home, no known drug allergies. Home medicines were listed but not yet reconciled at the time of this note.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:01: BP 147/88, HR 91, RR 21, T 98.7 F, SpO2 94% on room air. Tired-appearing but alert and conversant. Mucous membranes dry. Heart regular, lungs clear, abdomen soft and non-tender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 14:23: sodium 129 (L), potassium 6.0 (H), chloride 99, bicarbonate 16 (L), anion gap 14, urea nitrogen 38 (H), creatinine 5.44 (H), eGFR 12 (L), glucose 154 (H).</ref>

**Course in the ED.** Given the potassium result, calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given once, from 14:35 to 14:45. A 1000 mL bolus of 0.9% saline was started at 14:45, followed by 125 mL/h, and is still running.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with creatinine 5.44 and eGFR 12. Continue IV fluids and admit to the hospitalist service for monitoring and trending of renal function.
2. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.0, treated with IV calcium. Repeat potassium after fluids.

The hospitalist team has been called and the patient is being admitted.
