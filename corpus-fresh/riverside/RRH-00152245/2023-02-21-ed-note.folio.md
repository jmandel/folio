## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Vomiting, weakness and shortness of breath.

**History.** The patient is a woman with type 2 diabetes who presented with several days of nausea, vomiting and poor intake, now feeling weak and breathing fast. She takes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> at home; this has not yet been reconciled with her other home medications. Allergy list: lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** BP 109/65, HR 83, RR 18, T 99 F, SpO2 94% at 13:18. She appears ill and dehydrated, with dry mucous membranes and deep, rapid respirations. Abdomen is soft without guarding.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 13:41 shows sodium 134, potassium 5.5, chloride 95, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 31, urea nitrogen 40, creatinine 1.17, eGFR 47 and glucose 219</ref>. Venous blood gas pH 7.17, pCO2 26. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.9.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis</ref>: pH 7.17, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 31 and beta-hydroxybutyrate of 6.9 despite a glucose of only 219. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 14:22 and is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. Potassium is 5.5 on arrival; this will be watched closely as insulin is given.
3. Home metformin and losartan are not yet reconciled and are deferred to the admitting team.
