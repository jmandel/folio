## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, weakness and fast breathing.

**History.** The patient has type 2 diabetes and hypertension and came in after several days of poor oral intake, feeling weak and nauseated, with a sense of breathing hard. Home medications include <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily, semaglutide 0.5 mg weekly, losartan 100 mg daily and hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily; none have been reconciled yet in the ED. She has no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:18: BP 102/61, HR 97, RR 21, T 98.3 F, SpO2 94%. She looks dry and uncomfortable, with deep, rapid respirations. The abdomen is soft with mild diffuse discomfort and no guarding.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn 14:46: pH 7.11, pCO2 20. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.4. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 14:46: sodium 132, potassium 5.2, chloride 99, bicarbonate 12, anion gap 21, urea nitrogen 24, creatinine 1.36, eGFR 58, glucose 196.</ref>

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, with a severe acidosis and a wide anion gap despite a glucose under 200. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 15:22 and is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given the abdominal discomfort; lipase is pending at the time of this note.
3. Mild hyperkalemia and an elevated creatinine, likely from volume depletion; follow on repeat panels.
