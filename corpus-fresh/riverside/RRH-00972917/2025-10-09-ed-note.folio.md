## ED provider note

**History.** The patient is seen late on the evening of 10/9 with nausea, malaise and a sense of being short of breath. Symptoms have built over the last day or so. Allergy list: NKDA. Home medications are listed below and none has been reconciled yet in this visit: <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, semaglutide 0.5 mg weekly, losartan 100 mg daily, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:54: BP 105/63, HR 80, RR 21, T 98.8 F, SpO2 95%. The patient looks uncomfortable and dry, with deep, somewhat rapid respirations. Abdomen is soft with mild upper abdominal discomfort on palpation, no guarding.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn at 00:08: pH 7.12, pCO2 24. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.5 mmol/L on the same draw. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 00:08: sodium 131, potassium 5.2, chloride 98, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 25, urea nitrogen 25, creatinine 1.11, eGFR 61, glucose 227</ref>.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a severe anion gap acidosis with ketonemia while the glucose is only modestly raised. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 00:58 and is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">Rule out pancreatitis</ref> given the abdominal discomfort; further workup is pending.
3. Hyperkalemia and hyponatremia on the panel will be rechecked with the next chemistries. Creatinine is above what would be expected and will be followed.

The patient and family were updated on the diagnosis and the plan for admission.
