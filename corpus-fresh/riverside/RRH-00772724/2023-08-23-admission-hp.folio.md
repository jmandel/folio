## Admission history and physical

**Reason for admission:** euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis.

**History of present illness.** The patient presented to the ED this afternoon with nausea, malaise and fast breathing and was found to be markedly acidotic with a glucose of only 236. <ref id="precipitant">The ketoacidosis was precipitated by a weekend of heavy alcohol use with little food.</ref> She takes an SGLT2 inhibitor at home, as listed below. She has no known drug allergies (NKDA).

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:52: BP 122/73, HR 104, RR 17, T 98.3 F, SpO2 96%. She looks dry, with dry mucous membranes and mildly tachycardic. Lungs clear. Abdomen soft and nontender.

**Results.** On the 16:13 panel: sodium 134, potassium 5.5, chloride 98, bicarbonate 10, anion gap 26, urea nitrogen 32, creatinine 1.51 (eGFR 52), glucose 236. The insulin infusion per DKA protocol (0.1 units/kg/h) and D5 lactated Ringer's at 150 mL/h were started at 16:51 and are still running.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Euglycemic DKA, SGLT2 inhibitor-associated</ref>. Continue the insulin infusion with dextrose-containing fluid, since glucose is near normal. Recheck the metabolic panel serially until the gap closes. <ref id="med_empagliflozin">Empagliflozin is held.</ref>
2. Type 2 diabetes: <ref id="med_metformin">hold metformin</ref> while she is acidotic and NPO.
3. Hyperkalemia, potassium 5.5, with acute kidney injury, creatinine 1.51. <ref id="med_losartan">Hold losartan</ref> for volume depletion and potassium; resume when she is eating.
4. Volume depletion. <ref id="med_hydrochlorothiazide">Hold hydrochlorothiazide</ref> and <ref id="med_furosemide">hold furosemide</ref> for volume depletion; resume when able.
5. Other home medications (carvedilol, spironolactone, atorvastatin) continue.
