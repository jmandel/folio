## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department feeling unwell, with nausea, fatigue and rapid breathing. The home medication list includes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>; none of the home medications have been reconciled yet. Other home medications on the list are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, losartan 100 mg daily, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:11: BP 109/65, HR 109, RR 24, T 98.2 F, SpO2 98%. Tachycardic, with deep, rapid respirations. Mucous membranes are dry. Alert and answers questions appropriately.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn at 21:24: pH 7.08, pCO2 29 mmHg. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.7 mmol/L from the same draw. The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel at 21:24 showed sodium 134, potassium 5.4, chloride 99, bicarbonate 12, anion gap 23, urea nitrogen 30, creatinine 1.20, eGFR 67 and glucose 199</ref>.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The pH of 7.08, bicarbonate of 12 and anion gap of 23 with a beta-hydroxybutyrate of 5.7 establish ketoacidosis even though the glucose is only 199. An insulin regular infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 22:15 and is running, with dextrose 5% in lactated Ringer's at 150 mL/h started at the same time.
2. Potassium is 5.4 and creatinine is 1.20. These will be followed on repeat panels while insulin is running.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for continued DKA management. I discussed the findings with the patient, who understands the plan.
