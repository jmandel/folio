## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department this evening with several days of weakness and loose stools. Oral intake has been poor. No chest pain, no syncope, and no fever at home. There are no known drug allergies.

**Home medications.** Lisinopril 10 mg daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly. These have not yet been reconciled with the patient or pharmacy, and I am not making changes to them in the department.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:01: BP 124/74, HR 78, RR 19, T 99 F, SpO2 96%. The patient is alert and conversant, appears mildly dry, and is in no distress. Heart rhythm is regular. Lungs are clear. Abdomen is soft and nontender. No focal weakness on strength testing.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:12 shows sodium 130 (L), potassium 6.6 (HH), chloride 100, bicarbonate 23, anion gap 7, urea nitrogen 46 (H), creatinine 2.21 (H), eGFR 33 (L) and glucose 208 (H).</ref> The potassium is a critical value, and the bicarbonate and anion gap are reassuring against a significant acidosis.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref> with a potassium of 6.6. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 23:35 to 23:45 for membrane stabilization. Further monitoring and repeat potassium will be handled by the admitting team.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, creatinine 2.21 with urea nitrogen 46, in the setting of poor intake and diarrhea. Normal saline 1000 mL bolus was started at 23:45 and is running at 125 mL/h afterward.
3. Mild hyponatremia, sodium 130, expected to improve with volume repletion.

The patient is stable in the department and is being admitted to the hospitalist service for ongoing treatment and monitoring. The plan was discussed with the patient, who agrees.
