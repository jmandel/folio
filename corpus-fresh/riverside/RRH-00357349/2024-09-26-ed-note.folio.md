## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the emergency department with several days of cough, tiredness and feeling feverish. The cough has been productive, and the patient has felt a little short of breath with walking. No chest pain was reported. There is no known drug allergy (NKDA). Home medications, including metformin 500 mg twice daily and losartan 50 mg daily, have not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:26: BP 145/87, HR 78, RR 16, T 99 F, SpO2 98%. The patient looked comfortable and was speaking in full sentences. Breath sounds were diminished with crackles at the right base. Heart rhythm was regular. Abdomen was soft and nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="cbc">The CBC drawn at 18:49 showed white blood cells 13.1 (H), hemoglobin 12.6 and platelets 343</ref>. The basic metabolic panel from the same draw showed sodium 131 (L), potassium 4.8, chloride 97 (L), bicarbonate 20 (L), anion gap 14, urea nitrogen 47 (H), creatinine 1.17 (H), eGFR 46 (L) and glucose 129 (H). The picture is of a patient who is somewhat dry, with a mildly low sodium and an elevated urea nitrogen and creatinine.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>, based on the cough, the focal crackles on exam and the raised white count. Oxygen saturation is normal on room air and the patient is hemodynamically stable. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 19:50 and is running.
2. Low sodium and elevated urea nitrogen and creatinine, consistent with volume depletion. Normal saline at 75 mL/h was started at 19:10 and is still running. Repeat chemistry in the morning.
3. The patient will be admitted to the hospitalist service for continued treatment and monitoring. The patient understands and agrees with admission.
