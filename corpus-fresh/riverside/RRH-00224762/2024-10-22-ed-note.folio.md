## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is a man with diabetes, hypertension, hypothyroidism and kidney disease who presented with several days of productive cough, fever and shortness of breath that has been worse with walking. He has felt run down and has been eating poorly. He reports no chest pain, no vomiting and no leg swelling. He has an allergy to lisinopril, which caused angioedema. I have not reconciled his home medications in the ED.

**Exam.** Vitals at 15:40: BP 115/69, HR 78, RR 19, T 98.6 F, SpO2 95% on room air. He appears mildly ill but is not in distress. Breath sounds are decreased with crackles at the right base. Heart is regular. Abdomen is soft and nontender.

**Results.** The <ref id="cbc">blood count drawn at 16:05 showed white blood cells 16.7 (high), hemoglobin 12.3 and platelets 243</ref>. The basic metabolic panel from the same draw showed sodium 137, potassium 4.3, chloride 104, bicarbonate 18 (low), anion gap 15, urea nitrogen 31, creatinine 1.84, eGFR 34 and glucose 178. The chest film shows a right lower lobe airspace opacity.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>, with fever, cough, crackles and a focal opacity on film. He is not hypoxic on room air. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 17:04.
2. Low bicarbonate and elevated creatinine and urea nitrogen, in the setting of poor intake. Normal saline was started at 75 mL/h at 16:24 and the panel will be repeated.
3. He will be admitted to the hospitalist service for further treatment and monitoring.
