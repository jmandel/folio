## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, weakness and shortness of breath.

**History.** The patient is a man with type 2 diabetes who presented after several days of poor oral intake, nausea and malaise. He feels short of breath without chest pain. His home list includes <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, which has not yet been reconciled with him. He has no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** At 17:01: BP 112/67, HR 107, RR 22, T 99.1 F, SpO2 96%. He looks tired and dry, with deep respirations. Abdomen soft with mild diffuse discomfort and no guarding.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 17:14: sodium 132, potassium 4.9, chloride 98, bicarbonate 11, anion gap 23, BUN 34, creatinine 1.37, eGFR 49, glucose 156.</ref> Venous blood gas pH 7.11, pCO2 23. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.5.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: severe anion gap acidosis with ketosis despite a glucose of 156. Starting insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h with IV fluids, and admitting to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given nausea and abdominal discomfort; lipase is pending.
3. Remaining home medications are not yet reconciled; the admitting team will address them.
