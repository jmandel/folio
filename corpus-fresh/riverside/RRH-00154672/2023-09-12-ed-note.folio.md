## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with nausea and malaise and was noted to be breathing fast. On arrival at 17:55 the picture was concerning for a metabolic acidosis. Home medications are on the list below and have not yet been reconciled with the patient or pharmacy. Among them is <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>. The other listed home medications are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, dulaglutide 0.5 mg weekly, insulin glargine 14 units nightly, losartan 100 mg daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly. Allergy: lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:01: BP 98/59, HR 95, RR 22, T 98.3 F, SpO2 99%. Respirations were rapid and deep. The patient appeared tired and dry but was alert and conversant.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 18:26: sodium 135, potassium 5.5, chloride 95, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 32, urea nitrogen 26, creatinine 0.99, eGFR 64, glucose 244</ref>. Venous blood gas at 18:26 showed pH 7.20 and pCO2 25. Beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.3. The glucose is only modestly elevated for the degree of acidosis and ketosis.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, with a wide anion gap, low bicarbonate and a markedly elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate. An insulin infusion (regular insulin IV per DKA protocol, 0.1 units/kg/h) was started at 19:05 and is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> remains a working diagnosis given the presenting symptoms; lipase is pending and will be followed by the admitting team.
3. Potassium is 5.5 and will be rechecked on the repeat panels while on insulin.

Discussed with the patient, who agrees with admission.
