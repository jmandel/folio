## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department feeling weak and unwell after several days of loose stools with poor intake. There was no chest pain and no fainting. Home medication list shows rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly, which has not yet been reconciled. Allergies: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:01: BP 148/89, HR 112, RR 20, T 98.4 F, SpO2 96%. The patient appears tired and mildly dry. Heart is tachycardic and regular. Lungs are clear. Abdomen is soft and non-tender. No focal weakness.

**Results.** The basic metabolic panel drawn at 21:24 shows <ref id="bmp">sodium 137, potassium 6.1 (H), chloride 98, bicarbonate 20 (L), anion gap 19 (H), urea nitrogen 57 (H), creatinine 2.55 (H), eGFR 32 (L), glucose 169 (H)</ref>.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with a creatinine of 2.55 and urea nitrogen of 57 and an elevated anion gap. The patient looks volume depleted, so I started a 1000 mL bolus of normal saline at 21:45, now running at 125 mL/h.
2. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.1. I gave calcium gluconate 1 g IV from 21:35 to 21:45 to protect the heart while the potassium is addressed. The patient remained tachycardic but hemodynamically stable during the infusion.
3. The patient will be admitted to the hospitalist service for continued fluids, repeat chemistries and monitoring. The admitting hospitalist has been updated on the labs and the treatment given so far.

The patient and family understand the plan and agree with admission.
