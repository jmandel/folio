## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with malaise, nausea and rapid breathing. Vitals at 20:40: BP 117/70, HR 94, RR 20, T 98.1 F, SpO2 96%. Glucose was only modestly elevated despite marked acidosis, which points to <ref id="dx_dka">euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis</ref>. The patient takes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>; this has not yet been reconciled with the patient or pharmacy.

**Home medications (not yet reconciled).** Metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, dulaglutide 0.5 mg weekly, losartan 50 mg daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily, and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly.

**Allergies.** Lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** Uncomfortable but alert, with deep respirations. Mucous membranes are dry. Abdomen is soft.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel on the 20:55 draw showed sodium 132, potassium 4.9, chloride 100, bicarbonate 14, anion gap 18, urea nitrogen 35, creatinine 1.14, eGFR 58 and glucose 159</ref>. Venous blood gas pH 7.16, pCO2 23. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.9 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Euglycemic DKA with an elevated anion gap, low bicarbonate and a markedly elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate. The insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 21:44 and is still running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. Abdominal discomfort: <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">pancreatitis is still being ruled out</ref> and will be worked up by the admitting team.
3. Medication reconciliation is pending and will be completed by the admitting team.

Discussed with the patient, who agrees with admission.
