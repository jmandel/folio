## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the ED this afternoon with several hours of nausea, fatigue and rapid breathing. They take <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> at home; its status has not yet been reconciled. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** At 14:47: BP 98/59, HR 105, RR 16, T 99 F, SpO2 98%. Ill-appearing but alert and conversant. Mucous membranes are dry. Abdomen is soft without focal tenderness.

**Results.** The 15:00 panel: <ref id="bmp">sodium 130, potassium 5.6, chloride 97, bicarbonate 9, anion gap 24, urea nitrogen 31, creatinine 1.93, eGFR 29, glucose 184</ref>. Venous blood gas pH 7.15, pCO2 22. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.7.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: pH 7.15, bicarbonate 9, anion gap 24 and beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.7 with a glucose of only 184. Insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h, started 15:51, with IV fluids. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. Hyperkalemia, potassium 5.6, and acute-on-chronic reduction in kidney function with creatinine 1.93; both will be followed on repeat panels during the insulin infusion.
3. Home medications are not yet reconciled and will be addressed by the admitting team.
