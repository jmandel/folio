## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening feeling unwell, with nausea, marked fatigue and fast, deep breathing. There was no chest pain and no fever. Home medications on the list include <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily, losartan 100 mg daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly. None of these has been reconciled yet in the ED. Allergy: lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:40: BP 98/59, HR 100, RR 23, T 98.7 F, SpO2 97%. She appears fatigued and dry, with deep respirations. The abdomen is soft. She is alert and answers questions appropriately.

**Results.** On the 22:07 panel, the <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel showed sodium 130, potassium 4.9, chloride 99, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 23, urea nitrogen 40, creatinine 1.57, eGFR 38 and glucose 200</ref>. Venous blood gas pH 7.09, pCO2 21. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.3 mmol/L. The glucose is only modestly elevated despite the severe acidosis and the elevated ketones.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The wide anion gap, low bicarbonate, pH of 7.09 and beta-hydroxybutyrate of 6.3 establish the diagnosis even with a glucose of 200. An IV insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 22:44 and is still running. She will be admitted to the hospitalist service for continued care and serial chemistries.
2. Renal function: creatinine 1.57 and urea nitrogen 40 are elevated, consistent with volume depletion. Will follow with repeat panels.
3. Hyponatremia, sodium 130, to be rechecked with the next panel.

The case was discussed with the admitting hospitalist, who will assume care.
