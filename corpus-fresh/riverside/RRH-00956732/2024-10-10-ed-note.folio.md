## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department this evening feeling unwell, with several days of malaise, poor appetite and fatigue, and breathing that felt faster than usual. Home medications on the list are empagliflozin 10 mg daily, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, lisinopril 20 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly; none has been reconciled yet in the ED. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:26: BP 108/65, HR 106, RR 18, T 99.1 F, SpO2 96%. The patient looks tired and mildly dry, and is mildly tachycardic. Abdomen is soft. The patient is alert and answering questions appropriately.

**Results.** The blood glucose is only mildly elevated, but the acid-base picture is clearly abnormal. Venous blood gas drawn at 21:35 showed pH 7.11 and pCO2 29. Beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.9 mmol/L on the same draw. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 21:35: sodium 132, potassium 5.6 (H), chloride 97, bicarbonate 13, anion gap 22, urea nitrogen 28, creatinine 0.91, eGFR 71, glucose 159</ref>.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The patient has a marked anion gap metabolic acidosis with a high ketone level despite a glucose of 159. The home <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> is an important contributor to consider and has not been reconciled in the ED. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 22:30 and is still running. Admission to the hospitalist service for continued monitoring.
2. Potassium is high at 5.6, in the setting of acidosis. This will be rechecked as the acidosis is treated.
3. The remaining home medications (metformin, lisinopril, atorvastatin) are not yet reconciled and will be addressed by the admitting team.

The plan was discussed with the patient, who agrees with admission.
