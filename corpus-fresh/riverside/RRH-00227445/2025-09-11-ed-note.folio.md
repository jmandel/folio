## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Weakness, nausea and fast breathing.

**History.** The patient came in feeling unwell after several days of poor appetite and little to drink, with nausea and abdominal discomfort. They take <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> at home; the rest of the home medication list has not yet been reconciled. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** At 15:11 BP 104/62, HR 108, RR 22, temperature 98.4 F, SpO2 98%. The patient looks tired and mildly dehydrated, with dry mucous membranes and deep, rapid respirations. Abdomen is soft with mild upper abdominal discomfort and no guarding.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn 15:24: pH 7.22, pCO2 27. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.3. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 15:24: sodium 129, potassium 4.7, chloride 92, bicarbonate 14, anion gap 23, urea nitrogen 27, creatinine 1.22, eGFR 52, glucose 186.</ref> The glucose is only modestly raised despite a significant acidosis and a high ketone level.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, in a patient on an SGLT2 inhibitor. The pH is 7.22 with a gap of 23 and beta-hydroxybutyrate of 6.3. Plan is IV fluids and an insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h, with dextrose added because glucose is near normal. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">Pancreatitis is on the differential</ref> because of the abdominal discomfort; lipase is pending and the diagnosis is still being worked up.
3. Hyponatremia, sodium 129, and mild kidney function change, creatinine 1.22, probably related to volume loss. Recheck after fluids.

The patient and family were told about the diagnosis and the plan for admission, and they agree.
