## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening with chest pain. The pain was not accompanied by fainting, and the patient was comfortable and talking in full sentences on arrival. Home medications on the list are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals and lisinopril 10 mg daily; neither has been reconciled yet during this visit. The allergy list reads NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:11: BP 140/84, HR 86, RR 24, T 98.7 F, SpO2 99%. The patient appeared well, in no acute distress, with clear speech and good color. Heart rhythm was regular, and the chest wall and abdomen were soft on palpation. Respirations were somewhat quick at triage but unlabored on my examination.

**Results.** The first set of labs was drawn at 21:29. <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on that sample. A repeat was drawn at 00:29 on 3/17: <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. Basic metabolic panel from 21:29: sodium 138, potassium 4.5, chloride 105, bicarbonate 28, anion gap 5 (L), urea nitrogen 20, creatinine 0.78, eGFR 83, glucose 161 (H). The 12-lead ECG showed no ischemic changes.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The patient's pain has settled while in the department and the vital signs are reassuring. The mildly low anion gap and the mildly raised glucose on the panel are noted; kidney function is normal.
2. Disposition and follow-up: the results were reviewed with the patient, who understands that the heart enzymes were normal. Outpatient follow-up with the primary care physician within the next week is advised, and the patient should return for any recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting.
