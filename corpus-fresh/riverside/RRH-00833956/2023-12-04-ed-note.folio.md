## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, abdominal discomfort and rapid breathing.

**History.** The patient came in after several days of poor appetite, nausea and feeling short of breath on exertion. There was no chest pain and no fever at home. They take several diabetes medicines at home, including <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily, which has not yet been reconciled</ref>. Metformin, dulaglutide, insulin glargine and losartan are also on the home list and have not been reconciled yet. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 15:47: BP 116/70, HR 109, RR 17, T 98.3 F, SpO2 96%. The patient looks tired and dry, and is tachycardic with deep respirations. Abdomen is soft with mild diffuse tenderness and no guarding. Alert and oriented.

**Results.** Venous blood gas: pH 7.22, pCO2 29. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.4 mmol/L. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 16:12: sodium 132, potassium 4.6, chloride 98, bicarbonate 11, anion gap 23, urea nitrogen 27, creatinine 1.45, eGFR 39, glucose 158.</ref>

**ED course and assessment.** The picture is <ref id="dx_dka">diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a high anion gap acidosis with markedly elevated ketones despite a glucose of only 158. Insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 16:51 and is still running. Potassium is 4.6, so no replacement is needed now. Creatinine is above what would be expected and will be rechecked with the next panel. The patient is being admitted to the hospitalist service for continued insulin infusion and frequent labs.
