## ED provider note

**History.** The patient is seen in the emergency department with swelling of the lips and tongue that came on over the past several hours. There was no hives, no wheeze, and no trouble swallowing saliva, though speech feels a little thick. There was no new food, insect sting or new exposure. The only recent change is long-term blood pressure therapy, and the patient has taken it every morning as prescribed, including today. The patient has not had swelling like this before.

**Home medications.** Metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly are on the home list and have not yet been reconciled in the ED. The patient also takes <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 40 mg daily, which is stopped; the patient is told not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:54: BP 121/73, HR 83, RR 21, T 99.2 F, SpO2 94% on room air. The patient is anxious but sitting upright and speaking in full sentences. The lips and anterior tongue are visibly swollen. The floor of the mouth is soft, the uvula is midline, and there is no stridor. Lungs are clear. There is no rash. Heart rhythm is regular.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The picture fits swelling without hives or itching in a patient on a daily ACE inhibitor. The airway is currently patent, but swelling can progress, so the patient will be kept on continuous pulse oximetry and monitored closely with the airway team aware. Oxygen saturation is slightly low at 94%, and we will watch it closely. The patient and family were told clearly that this medication class must not be taken again, and that any future swelling of the face, lips or tongue needs an immediate return to the emergency department.
2. Disposition: the patient will be admitted for airway observation. The hospitalist team has been called.
3. Remaining home medications will be reconciled by the admitting team.
