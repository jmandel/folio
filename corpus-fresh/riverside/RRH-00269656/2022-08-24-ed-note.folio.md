## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presents with four days of cough with yellow sputum, fever and chills at home, and shortness of breath on exertion. There was some pain on the right side of the chest with deep breaths. Appetite has been poor. She has not had vomiting or diarrhea.

**Allergies:** lisinopril (angioedema).

**Home medications.** Metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, amlodipine 10 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly are on the home list and have not yet been reconciled in the ED.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:11: BP 135/81, HR 102, RR 21, T 98 F, SpO2 98%. She looks tired but is not in distress. Breath sounds are decreased with crackles at the right base. Heart is tachycardic and regular. Abdomen is soft. Mucous membranes are slightly dry.

**Results.** Basic metabolic panel drawn 18:24: sodium 135, potassium 4.2, chloride 100, bicarbonate 22, anion gap 13, urea nitrogen 33, creatinine 1.70, eGFR 43, glucose 192. <ref id="cbc">The blood count on the same draw shows white blood cells 17.0, hemoglobin 12.8 and platelets 239</ref>. The raised white count supports an infectious process. Creatinine is elevated, consistent with volume depletion from poor intake and fever.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 19:35. She is hemodynamically stable and oxygenating well at rest.
2. Dehydration with raised creatinine: normal saline at 75 mL/h was started at 18:55 and continues.
3. Disposition: admit to the hospitalist service for IV antibiotics, fluids and repeat labs in the morning. The patient and family agree with the plan.
