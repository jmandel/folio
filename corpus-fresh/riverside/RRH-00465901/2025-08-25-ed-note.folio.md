## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, malaise and fast breathing.

**History.** The patient is a woman with type 2 diabetes who came in feeling weak and nauseated, with deep, rapid breathing. Her home medications include <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>. She has no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:26: BP 104/62, HR 111, RR 20, T 99.1 F, SpO2 98%. She appears uncomfortable and tired. Mucous membranes are dry, and she is tachycardic with a regular rhythm. Lungs are clear. Abdomen is soft.

**Results.** Venous blood gas at 18:36: pH 7.13, pCO2 30. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.7 mmol/L. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel: sodium 132, potassium 4.6, chloride 93, bicarbonate 13, anion gap 26, urea nitrogen 36, creatinine 1.17, eGFR 54, glucose 227.</ref>

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic.</ref> She has a profound anion gap metabolic acidosis with a markedly elevated ketone level, yet her glucose is only modestly raised. This picture fits ketoacidosis in a patient on an SGLT2 inhibitor. Start an IV insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h with dextrose-containing fluids so that glucose does not fall too low. Insulin and fluids were started at 19:30.
2. Mild hyponatremia and a creatinine of 1.17, likely from volume depletion. Recheck with serial chemistries.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for continued insulin infusion and frequent labs.
