## Discharge summary

**Diagnosis:** <ref id="dx_dka">Euglycemic DKA, SGLT2 inhibitor-associated, resolved.</ref>

**Hospital course.** The patient presented with nausea, fatigue and rapid breathing and was found to have a severe anion gap acidosis with a glucose of only 227 on arrival. She was treated with an insulin infusion and dextrose-containing fluids, then transitioned to subcutaneous insulin. No clear precipitant was identified: no infection, no missed meals reported, no alcohol, no recent surgery. Her symptoms cleared and she is eating.

**Discharge exam and results.** She is comfortable and alert. Vitals at 09:30: BP 122/73, HR 109, RR 19, T 99 F, SpO2 97%. On the 06:02 panel on 8/27: sodium 137, potassium 4.4, chloride 99, bicarbonate 19, anion gap 19, urea nitrogen 21, creatinine 0.89, eGFR 75, glucose 134. Her kidney function is back to normal.

**Allergies:** no known drug allergies.

**Discharge medications**

- Dapagliflozin 10 mg daily: <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">do not resume until reviewed by her PCP.</ref> This is the likely cause of the ketoacidosis, and she was counseled about it.
- <ref id="med_metformin">Metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals: continue.</ref>
- Glipizide 5 mg daily: continue.
- Dulaglutide 0.5 mg subcutaneously weekly: continue.
- Insulin glargine 10 units subcutaneously nightly: continue.
- Lisinopril 20 mg daily: continue.
- Rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly: continue.

**Follow-up.** Primary care at Northgate Family Medicine within 1 week to decide whether to restart dapagliflozin and to recheck a basic metabolic panel. She should return to the ED for vomiting, abdominal pain, fast breathing or marked weakness.
