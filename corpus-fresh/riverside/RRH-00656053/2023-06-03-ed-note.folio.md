## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening with swelling of the lips and lower face that came on over the past several hours. There was no rash and no difficulty swallowing, and the patient was able to speak in full sentences and handle secretions. Home medications include lisinopril 20 mg daily, and the swelling fits the pattern of a reaction to that drug. The allergy list on arrival read NKDA, so this is a new reaction. Metformin and amlodipine are also on the home list and have not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:54: BP 124/74, HR 80, RR 17, T 98.6 F, SpO2 95% on the monitor. Comfortable, speaking clearly, no stridor or drooling. Lips and perioral tissue visibly swollen; tongue and floor of mouth not involved on inspection. Lungs clear. No hives.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The airway is currently patent and oxygen saturation is acceptable, but the swelling can progress, so the airway will be watched closely with repeat checks of the voice, tongue and breathing. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 21:13. The patient was told clearly that this reaction is serious and may come back if the drug is taken again.
2. ACE inhibitor: <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 20 mg daily is stopped; do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. This will be added to the allergy list so that it is visible to every clinician who sees the patient.
3. Remaining home medications will be reconciled by the admitting team.

The patient and family understood the explanation and agreed with the plan. Return precautions and airway concerns were reviewed, and the patient will be reassessed in the department as the swelling evolves.
