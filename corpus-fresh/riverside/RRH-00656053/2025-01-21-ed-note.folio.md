## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient came in tonight feeling very unwell, with nausea, weakness and fast, deep breathing. Home medications on the list are <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, losartan 50 mg daily and amlodipine 10 mg daily; none of these has been reconciled yet. Allergy: lisinopril, which caused angioedema.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:40: BP 110/66, HR 107, RR 19, T 98.8 F, SpO2 98%. Uncomfortable but alert and oriented. Dry mucous membranes, mildly tachycardic, breathing somewhat deep. Abdomen soft, without guarding.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn 00:01 showed sodium 128, potassium 5.3, chloride 96, bicarbonate 9, anion gap 23, urea nitrogen 33, creatinine 1.00, eGFR 60 and glucose 188</ref>. Venous blood gas on the same draw: pH 7.15, pCO2 28. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.2 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The glucose is only mildly elevated, but the wide anion gap, low bicarbonate, acidemia and markedly raised ketones establish the diagnosis. The patient is on an SGLT2 inhibitor, which is the likely setting for euglycemic presentation. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started 00:44 and is still running. Admission to the hospitalist service.
2. Hyponatremia and mild hyperkalemia are noted on the panel and will be followed with repeat chemistries on the infusion.
3. Medication reconciliation is deferred to the admitting team.

The patient and family were told the diagnosis and the reason for admission.
