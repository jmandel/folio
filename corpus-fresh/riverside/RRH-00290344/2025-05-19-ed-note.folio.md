## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening feeling unwell, with rapid breathing and marked fatigue. Home medications are listed in the chart, but the list has not yet been reconciled. Among them is <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily, not yet reconciled</ref>. Allergies: no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:11: BP 108/65, HR 80, RR 24, temperature 99 F, SpO2 95%. The patient appears fatigued and is breathing deeply and quickly. Mucous membranes are somewhat dry. The abdomen is soft. The patient is alert and answers questions appropriately.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 19:37 shows sodium 134 (L), potassium 4.9, chloride 98, bicarbonate 12 (L), anion gap 24 (H), urea nitrogen 24 (H), creatinine 1.27 (H), eGFR 49 (L), and glucose 239 (H)</ref>. Venous blood gas at 19:37: pH 7.23 (L), pCO2 22 (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.3 mmol/L, drawn 19:37.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>. The glucose is only modestly raised, but the low bicarbonate, wide anion gap, acidic venous pH and elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate establish the diagnosis. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 20:15 and is still running. Continue to monitor the metabolic panel and ketones closely.
2. Admit to the hospitalist service for continued treatment and monitoring. The patient was told the reason for admission and agrees with the plan.
