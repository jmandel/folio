## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with several days of productive cough, tiredness and mild shortness of breath. Intake of food and fluids has been poor. No chest pain was reported. Allergies: none known. Home medications were listed but not yet reconciled in the ED.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:54: BP 147/88, HR 105, RR 21, T 98.3 F, SpO2 95%. Ill-appearing but alert and conversant. Mildly tachypneic with crackles at the base on the affected side. Heart tachycardic and regular. Abdomen soft. No leg swelling.

**Results.** Labs were drawn at 17:15. <ref id="cbc">White blood cells 16.3, hemoglobin 13.1, platelets 303</ref>, so there is a clear leukocytosis. Basic metabolic panel: sodium 134, potassium 4.3, chloride 97, bicarbonate 23, anion gap 14, urea nitrogen 56, creatinine 1.69, eGFR 38, glucose 148. The urea nitrogen and creatinine are elevated, consistent with volume depletion.

**ED course.** Normal saline was started at 75 mL/h at 17:38 and is still running. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 18:18. Heart rate and work of breathing were improving with fluids when I last checked.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>, with fever absent but a high white count, tachycardia and cough. Continue ceftriaxone and IV fluids. Admit to the hospitalist service for further treatment and observation.
2. Elevated urea nitrogen and creatinine, likely from poor intake. Continue fluids and repeat the panel in the morning.
3. Home medications will be reconciled by the admitting team.
