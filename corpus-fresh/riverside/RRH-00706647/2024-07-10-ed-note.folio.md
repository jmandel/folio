## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, malaise and fast breathing.

**History.** The patient has type 2 diabetes and hypertension and came to the ED feeling unwell, with nausea and a sense of being unable to catch their breath. Home medications include <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, losartan 50 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly. Medication reconciliation has not been completed in the ED. Allergy: lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:54: BP 101/61, HR 97, RR 24, temperature 99 F, SpO2 94%. Ill-appearing, with deep rapid respirations. Mucous membranes are dry.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn 15:07: pH 7.22, pCO2 26. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.9. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 15:07: sodium 130, potassium 4.7, chloride 97, bicarbonate 12, anion gap 21, urea nitrogen 35, creatinine 1.51, eGFR 50, glucose 218.</ref>

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis</ref>: acidemia with a widened anion gap, markedly elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate and a glucose of only 218, in a patient on an SGLT2 inhibitor. Started the insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h at 15:58 and it is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> as a cause of the presentation; this remains under evaluation.
3. Creatinine 1.51 and urea nitrogen 35 suggest volume depletion; fluids are part of the DKA protocol.
