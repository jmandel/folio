## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the ED this evening feeling unwell, with nausea, fatigue and general weakness. Allergies: NKDA. Home medications as listed on arrival, not yet reconciled: <ref id="med_dapagliflozin">dapagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, losartan 100 mg daily, carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, furosemide 40 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:11: BP 114/68, HR 94, RR 17, T 98.8 F, SpO2 99%. The patient looks mildly dry and uncomfortable but is alert and conversant. Abdomen soft with some vague upper abdominal discomfort.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 21:39: sodium 131 (L), potassium 5.2 (H), chloride 99, bicarbonate 10 (LL), anion gap 22 (H), urea nitrogen 31 (H), creatinine 1.63 (H), eGFR 51 (L), glucose 215 (H)</ref>. Venous blood gas at 21:39: pH 7.13 (LL), pCO2 28 (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.3 mmol/L, drawn 21:39.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a wide anion gap acidosis with a low bicarbonate and an elevated ketone level, while glucose is only modestly raised. The insulin infusion per DKA protocol (regular insulin 0.1 units/kg/h) was started at 22:15 and is running. The patient will need admission to the hospital service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given the upper abdominal discomfort; this remains a working consideration while the work-up continues.
3. Potassium is high and creatinine is raised; both will be followed with repeat chemistries.

The hospitalist team has been asked to admit.
