## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Abnormal labs and feeling unwell, found to have a high potassium.

**History.** The patient was seen this afternoon and describes feeling run down. Home medications on the list are metformin 1000 mg twice daily, glipizide 5 mg daily, losartan 50 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly; none have been reconciled in the ED yet. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:40: BP 115/69, HR 90, RR 22, T 98.9 F, SpO2 97%. Alert and conversant, in no acute distress. Lungs clear, heart regular, abdomen soft and nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 16:58: sodium 137, potassium 6.7 (critical), chloride 106, bicarbonate 17, anion gap 14, urea nitrogen 52, creatinine 2.57, eGFR 22, glucose 125</ref>.

**ED course.** Given the potassium, calcium gluconate 1 g IV was started at 17:14 and finished at 17:24 as cardiac membrane protection. At 17:24 a 1000 mL bolus of 0.9% sodium chloride was started, followed by 125 mL/h, which is still running.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with creatinine 2.57 and urea nitrogen 52. Continue IV saline and admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.7, treated with IV calcium. Recheck potassium after fluids and monitor on telemetry.
3. Admit to medicine; the hospitalist will see the patient and decide on the home medications.
