## Emergency department provider note

**Reason for visit:** chest pain.

**History.** The patient came to the ED this afternoon with chest pain and was seen at 18:34. The pain was described as a pressure-like discomfort in the chest. There was no fainting. The patient is allergic to nothing that we know of: the allergy list reads NKDA. Home medications on the list are losartan 50 mg by mouth daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg by mouth nightly; neither has been reconciled yet in this visit.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:40: BP 154/92, HR 106, RR 18, T 98.9 F, SpO2 97%. The patient was awake, conversant and in no acute distress. Heart rhythm was regular and rate mildly fast. Lungs were clear and breathing was unlabored. The abdomen was soft. No leg swelling.

**Results.** ECG showed no ischemic changes. Basic metabolic panel drawn at 19:05: sodium 140, potassium 4.5, chloride 102, bicarbonate (total CO2) 23, anion gap 15, urea nitrogen 18, creatinine 1.06, eGFR (CKD-EPI 2021) 84, glucose 122 (H). The first cardiac marker, <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL on the 19:05 draw</ref>, was normal. The repeat, <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL on the 22:05 draw</ref>, was also normal.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The heart rate and blood pressure were somewhat high on arrival, which is noted for follow-up with the patient's own physician.
2. Chemistries are unremarkable apart from the mildly raised glucose of 122; kidney function is normal with creatinine 1.06.
3. The results were reviewed with the patient, who was reassured. Return precautions were given for any recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting, and the patient was asked to see their primary care physician within the week.
