## Emergency department provider note

**Reason for visit:** chest pain.

**History.** The patient came to the ED on the afternoon of 7/10 with chest pain. They described it as a pressure-like discomfort in the center of the chest that came on at rest and eased over time. There was some mild shortness of breath with it. No fainting and no leg swelling. They have no known drug allergies. Home medications on the list are losartan 100 mg daily and amlodipine 5 mg daily; these have not been reconciled in the ED.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:01: BP 142/85, HR 110, RR 21, temperature 99 F, SpO2 97%. The patient was uncomfortable but spoke in full sentences. Heart rhythm was regular and fast, with no murmur. Lungs were clear. The chest wall was not tender. The abdomen was soft. No leg edema.

**Results.** An ECG was obtained on arrival and showed no ischemic changes. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 18:12 showed sodium 140, potassium 4.4, chloride 99, bicarbonate 23, anion gap 18 (H), urea nitrogen 20, creatinine 1.04, eGFR 79 and glucose 126 (H). The anion gap is mildly elevated with a normal bicarbonate, and we will note it for the primary team and for follow-up. Troponin I was <0.01 ng/mL on the 18:12 draw and was repeated at 21:12.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: <ref id="trop_1">troponin I <0.01 at 18:12</ref> and <ref id="trop_2">again <0.01 at 21:12</ref>, three hours apart, and an ECG without ischemic changes. The pain settled during the evening in the department and the heart rate came down from its initial value.
2. The patient was told the cardiac workup was reassuring. They should return for any recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or sweating with the pain, and should follow up with their primary care physician within the next week to review blood pressure and the repeat chemistry.
