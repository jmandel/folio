## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient came to the emergency department with chest pain. The pain has settled and they were comfortable and talking in full sentences when I saw them. There was no fainting and no report of ongoing shortness of breath. Allergy list: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:40: BP 155/93, HR 79, RR 18, T 98.3 F, SpO2 95%. Comfortable, in no distress. Heart rate regular, lungs clear bilaterally, chest wall without reproducible tenderness on my exam. Abdomen soft. No leg swelling.

**Results.** ECG showed no ischemic changes. The first troponin was drawn at 15:06: <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The repeat was drawn at 18:06: <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The basic metabolic panel on the 15:06 draw showed sodium 136, potassium 4.6, chloride 101, bicarbonate 27, anion gap 8, urea nitrogen 23, creatinine 0.97 with eGFR 87, and glucose 161 (H). Kidney function is normal and electrolytes are unremarkable; the mildly raised glucose needs no action in the department.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The pain resolved during the stay and the patient remained stable with normal vital signs apart from a blood pressure that was elevated on arrival.
2. Disposition: the patient is appropriate for discharge home. I reviewed the results with them and explained that the testing was reassuring. They should follow up with their primary care physician within the next week, including a repeat blood pressure check. They should return promptly for recurrent or worsening chest pain, pain with exertion, shortness of breath, fainting, or any new symptom that worries them.
