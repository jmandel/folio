## Emergency department provider note

**Reason for visit:** swelling of the lips and face.

**History.** The patient came in this afternoon after noticing that his lips and the lower part of his face had become swollen over the course of the day. There was no rash and no itching. He described a thick feeling in the mouth but said he was able to swallow his saliva and had no trouble breathing at rest. He has not had a reaction like this before. He takes <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 40 mg once daily, which is stopped today with the instruction not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. He also takes metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly; both are listed from his home medications and have not yet been reconciled. He has no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:01: BP 140/84, HR 82, RR 22, T 98.1 F, SpO2 96%. He is alert and speaking in full sentences. The lips and chin are visibly swollen without hives. The respiratory rate is slightly high, but he is comfortable sitting upright, and his voice is clear. Lungs are clear to auscultation. The airway is being watched closely.

**Treatment.** Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 18:20.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling is without hives or itching, in a patient on a long-standing ACE inhibitor, which fits a bradykinin-mediated process rather than an allergic one. He has no known drug allergies. Methylprednisolone has been given as above. He will stay under observation in the department with repeated airway checks, and he has been told to report any change in voice, tongue swelling, or difficulty swallowing or breathing right away.
2. Medications: lisinopril is stopped as above, and he and his family have been told clearly that he should not take an ACE inhibitor again. Metformin and rosuvastatin remain on his home list and are not reconciled at this visit.

Disposition will be decided after the period of observation, and the admitting team will be updated if the swelling progresses.
