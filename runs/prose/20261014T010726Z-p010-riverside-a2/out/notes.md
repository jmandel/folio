## Emergency department provider note

**Reason for visit:** swelling of the lips and tongue.

**History.** The patient came to the ED this afternoon after noticing progressive swelling of the lips and tongue over the past several hours. There was no rash and no itching. They described a thick feeling in the mouth and some difficulty speaking clearly, but no trouble swallowing saliva and no wheeze. They have not had swelling like this before. At home they take lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily. Allergy list: no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:11: BP 146/88, HR 102, RR 24, T 98.4 F, SpO2 95%. The patient is anxious but speaking in full sentences. There is marked swelling of the lower lip and the tongue, with mild swelling of the floor of the mouth. The uvula is visible and the oropharynx is not occluded. No stridor. Lungs are clear bilaterally. No hives or other skin findings.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The picture of lip and tongue swelling without hives or itching, in a patient taking lisinopril, fits this diagnosis. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 18:30. The airway was assessed at the bedside and is currently patent, but the swelling involves the tongue, so the patient will stay under close observation with repeated airway checks and continuous pulse oximetry.
2. <ref id="med_lisinopril">Lisinopril is stopped. Do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again.</ref> I explained that this reaction can return with any drug in the same class, and the patient voiced understanding.

The patient will be reassessed after the steroid has had time to work, and the plan for disposition will be set on that reassessment.
