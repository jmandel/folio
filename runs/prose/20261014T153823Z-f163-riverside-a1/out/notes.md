## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the ED this evening with swelling of the lips and lower face that developed over the last several hours. They describe the lips as tight and heavy. They report no hives, no itching, and no difficulty breathing or swallowing. They take <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily, which is stopped; the patient is told not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. They also take atorvastatin 40 mg nightly, which has not yet been reconciled. Allergies: no known drug allergies. They have not had swelling like this before.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:26: BP 118/71, HR 94, RR 18, temperature 98.1 F, SpO2 96%. The patient is alert, speaking in full sentences, and handling their own secretions. The lips and the lower face are visibly swollen. There is no stridor and no wheeze, and breathing is unlabored. Skin shows no urticaria. The lungs are clear to auscultation. Heart rate is regular.

**Course in the ED.** Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 21:45. The airway is being watched closely with repeated reassessment at the bedside, and the patient was told to tell the nurse right away if the throat feels tight, the voice changes, or swallowing becomes harder.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling is limited to the lips and lower face, the airway is currently patent, and there are no hives to suggest an allergic cause. Lisinopril is stopped and the patient has been counseled not to take any ACE inhibitor again. Continue airway monitoring and reassess the swelling after the steroid dose.
2. Atorvastatin was not reconciled during this visit; it will be addressed at the time of admission or discharge medication review.

The patient understands the plan and agrees with it.
