## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening with swelling of the lips and face that came on over several hours. There was no rash and no itching. They described a thick feeling in the mouth but said they were swallowing their saliva and breathing without difficulty. They have no prior episodes like this. Their home medication list includes <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 40 mg daily, which is stopped; the instruction given is "Do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again"</ref>. The remaining home medications are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily (half of a 25 mg tablet) and amlodipine 5 mg daily; none of these has been reconciled yet in the ED. Allergy list: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 21:47: BP 143/86, HR 95, RR 24, T 99.1 F, SpO2 99%. The patient is anxious but speaking in full sentences, with no stridor and no drooling. There is visible swelling of the lips and lower face. Lungs are clear bilaterally with comfortable work of breathing. Heart rhythm is regular. Skin shows no hives.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling without hives or itching, in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor, fits this picture rather than an allergic reaction. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV once was ordered, started 22:06. The patient is being watched closely with continuous pulse oximetry, and the airway will be rechecked frequently, since swelling of this kind can progress. The patient and family were told clearly why lisinopril must not be restarted, and the instruction will be written on the discharge paperwork and the allergy record.
2. Home medications: the other listed medications remain to be reconciled by the admitting team.

Disposition will depend on how the swelling behaves over the next several hours of observation.
