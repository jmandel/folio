## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the emergency department this afternoon with chest pain. The discomfort was central, came on at rest, and was not clearly related to exertion or to breathing. There was no fainting, and no leg swelling was reported. Allergy list: lisinopril (cough). Home medications on the list (metformin, losartan, chlorthalidone, atorvastatin) have not been reconciled in the ED.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:47: BP 160/96, HR 105, RR 16, T 98.9 F, SpO2 98%. The patient was comfortable at rest and spoke in full sentences. Heart rhythm was regular with no murmur appreciated. Lungs were clear. The chest wall was not tender. The abdomen was soft, and the legs were not swollen.

**Results.** <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on the 17:08 panel, and <ref id="trop_2">troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on the repeat drawn at 20:08. The ECG showed no ischemic changes. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 17:08: sodium 138, potassium 4.6, chloride 102, bicarbonate 28, anion gap 8, urea nitrogen 12, creatinine 1.23 (H), eGFR 46 (L), glucose 155 (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The pain did not recur during the stay in the department.
2. Creatinine 1.23 with eGFR 46 on the 17:08 panel; electrolytes and bicarbonate were otherwise unremarkable.
3. Blood pressure and heart rate were elevated at triage at 16:47, in the setting of pain and the visit itself. They should be rechecked at outpatient follow-up.

The results were reviewed with the patient, who was reassured. Return precautions were given for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting. Follow-up with the primary care physician within the week.
