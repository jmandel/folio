## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the ED with swelling of the lips and face that developed over the course of the day, with a sensation of fullness in the mouth. There was no rash and no itching. The patient takes <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 10 mg by mouth daily</ref> for blood pressure and has been on it for some time. The patient has no known drug allergies on the allergy list. Other home medications on the list, metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily and one further daily tablet, have not yet been reconciled with the patient or pharmacy.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:18: BP 150/90, HR 79, RR 16, T 98.9 F, SpO2 95%. The patient was sitting upright, speaking in full sentences, and not in distress. There was visible swelling of the lips with mild facial puffiness. Voice was normal, there was no stridor, and the patient was handling secretions. Lungs were clear. Skin showed no hives.

**Course in the ED.** Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 14:37. The airway was watched closely and did not worsen while in the department.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The picture of lip and facial swelling without hives or itching, in a patient taking lisinopril, fits this diagnosis. The airway is currently patent. The patient was told to stop the drug: do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again. This was explained in plain words, and the patient voiced understanding.
2. Blood pressure was mildly elevated at 150/90 on arrival, likely in the setting of the acute event and anxiety. It will be rechecked.
3. Home medication reconciliation remains to be completed with the patient and pharmacy.

The patient will remain under observation with repeat airway checks, and the admitting team will be updated on any change in the swelling.
