## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this afternoon with swelling of the lips and face that she noticed earlier today. She describes the lips as feeling tight and thick. She has had no hives, no wheezing, and no trouble swallowing or handling her saliva. She has not had a reaction like this before and has no known drug allergies. Her home medications include <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 40 mg tablet, 40 mg by mouth daily, now stopped: do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily and levothyroxine 75 mcg daily are also on her home list; neither has been reconciled yet in the ED.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:11: BP 116/70, HR 92, RR 18, T 98.4 F, SpO2 98%. She is comfortable, speaks in full sentences, and is not drooling. There is visible swelling of the lips with mild fullness of the lower face. The voice is normal, there is no stridor, and breathing is unlabored. Lungs are clear. There is no rash.

**Results.** No laboratory results are back at the time of this note.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling is limited to the lips and lower face with a patent airway and normal oxygen saturation, in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor and without hives. This pattern fits an ACE inhibitor reaction rather than an allergic one. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 16:30. She will stay under close observation in the department with repeated airway checks, and I have asked the nurses to call me right away for any change in voice, trouble swallowing, or noisy breathing. She and her family were told that the ACE inhibitor must not be taken again, and this will be written clearly in her discharge or admission instructions.
2. Home medications: the remaining home list is as above and will be reconciled with the admitting or follow-up team.

Disposition will be decided after the observation period and reassessment of the swelling.
