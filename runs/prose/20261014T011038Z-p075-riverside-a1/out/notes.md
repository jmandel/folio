## Emergency department provider note

**Reason for visit:** chest pain.

**History.** The patient presented to the ED on the evening of 11/23 with chest pain. I saw them at 18:41. They are allergic to nothing that they know of; the allergy list reads NKDA. Their home medication list includes rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly, which has not yet been reconciled against what they actually take.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:47: BP 156/94, HR 110, RR 19, temperature 98.7 F, SpO2 95%. The patient was awake and talking in full sentences, in no acute distress. The heart was tachycardic but regular. Lungs were clear and breathing was unlabored.

**Results.** Basic metabolic panel drawn at 19:10: sodium 138, potassium 4.6, chloride 100, bicarbonate 26, anion gap 12, urea nitrogen 21, creatinine 1.10, eGFR 73, glucose 141 (H). Electrolytes and kidney function are unremarkable. The first set of cardiac markers, on the 19:10 draw, showed <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The repeat on the 22:10 draw showed <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The ECG showed no ischemic changes.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The mild tachycardia and elevated blood pressure on arrival were noted and are reassuring in the context of a negative cardiac evaluation.
2. Glucose of 141 on the panel is mildly elevated and was noted for outpatient follow-up.
3. Disposition: the patient was told the results of the cardiac evaluation and was advised to follow up with their primary care physician within the week. They should return for any recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting.
