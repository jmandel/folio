## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the ED this afternoon with chest pain. The discomfort was brought on without clear exertion and was not pleasant to describe but was not crushing; there was no fainting. They were seen and examined at 15:34. Home medications are listed below from the intake record and have not yet been reconciled: metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, lisinopril 20 mg daily, and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly. Allergies: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 15:40: BP 150/90, HR 112, RR 19, T 98.9 F, SpO2 98%. The patient was awake, conversant, and in no acute distress. Heart rhythm was regular and fast, with no murmur. Lungs were clear bilaterally. The chest wall was not tender to palpation. Extremities were warm without swelling.

**Results.** The 12-lead ECG showed no ischemic changes. <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on the 15:57 draw, and <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref> on the repeat at 18:57. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 15:57 showed sodium 141, potassium 4.5, chloride 100, bicarbonate 25, anion gap 16, urea nitrogen 15, creatinine 1.10, eGFR 83, and glucose 122 (H).

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The pain did not behave like a cardiac event during the observation period, and the kidney function and electrolytes on the panel were reassuring.
2. The heart rate was elevated on arrival; this was noted and rechecked during the stay.
3. Disposition: the patient is stable for discharge from the ED with outpatient primary care follow-up within the next week. Return precautions were reviewed: recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or any new concerning symptom should bring them back right away.
