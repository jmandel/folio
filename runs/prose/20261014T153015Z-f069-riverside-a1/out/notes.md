## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the emergency department with chest pain. The discomfort was a pressure-like sensation in the chest that was not clearly related to exertion. There was no fainting and no leg swelling. They were comfortable on arrival and able to give a clear history.

**Home medications.** The home medication list shows losartan 100 mg daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly. None of these has been reconciled yet. **Allergies:** NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:40: BP 140/84, HR 104, RR 16, T 98.2 F, SpO2 96%. The patient looked well and was in no distress. Lungs were clear and breathing was unlabored. Heart sounds were regular without a murmur. The chest wall was non-tender, and the abdomen was soft.

**Results.** The first troponin was drawn at 17:05: <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The repeat was drawn at 20:05: <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The basic metabolic panel on the 17:05 draw showed sodium 137, potassium 4.2, chloride 104, bicarbonate 28, anion gap 5 (L), urea nitrogen 14, creatinine 1.36 (H), eGFR 48 (L) and glucose 157 (H). The ECG showed no ischemic changes.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_acs">Chest pain, acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>. Acute coronary syndrome ruled out: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The pain did not recur during the observation period, and vital signs remained stable.
2. Creatinine 1.36 with eGFR 48 on the 17:05 panel; electrolytes were otherwise unremarkable. Outpatient follow-up of the kidney function is advised.
3. Follow-up with the primary care physician within the next week was discussed with the patient, along with return precautions for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath or fainting. The patient voiced understanding.
