## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening with swelling of the lips and the lower face that came on over the course of the day. There was no hives and no itching. They described a tight, full feeling in the lips but said their breathing and swallowing felt normal. No one else at home has been ill, and they could not point to a new food or exposure. They take <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 40 mg daily, which is now stopped; the instruction is not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Other home medications on their list are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly; these have not yet been reconciled. Allergy list: no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:01: BP 120/72, HR 98, RR 17, T 98.6 F, SpO2 99%. The patient was sitting comfortably and speaking in full sentences. The lips and lower face were visibly swollen without a rash. The voice was clear, there was no stridor, and breathing was unlabored. Lungs were clear to auscultation. The heart rate was regular.

**Course.** I watched the airway closely while the patient was in the department. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 20:20. The patient stayed comfortable, with oxygen saturation holding at 99% and no change in voice or breathing during my evaluation.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling without hives or itching, in a patient taking lisinopril, fits this picture rather than an allergic reaction. The patient and family were told clearly that lisinopril and every other ACE inhibitor must be avoided from now on, and this was written on the discharge paperwork and the medication list. Airway status will be rechecked repeatedly, and the patient was told to return immediately for any trouble breathing, swallowing, or speaking, or for tongue or throat swelling.
2. Home medications: the remaining home medications are listed above and will be reconciled by the team that follows the patient.
