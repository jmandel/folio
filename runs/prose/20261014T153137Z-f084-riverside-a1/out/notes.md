## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented on the afternoon of 8/2 with swelling of the lips and tongue that came on over the past several hours. They describe a thick feeling of the tongue and some difficulty speaking clearly, without hives, and no new foods or insect stings. They take <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily, which is now stopped; the patient was told not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Other home medications on the list are metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, glipizide 5 mg daily, insulin glargine 10 units subcutaneously nightly, and hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily. These have not yet been reconciled with the patient or pharmacy. Allergy list: no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:47: BP 118/71, HR 93, RR 23, T 99.1 F, SpO2 97%. Alert and speaking in full sentences, though the voice is slightly muffled. Visible swelling of the lips and tongue. The patient is handling secretions and there is no stridor. Lungs are clear. No rash.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The picture fits swelling from the ACE inhibitor rather than an allergic reaction, given the absence of hives and of any new exposure. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 17:06. The airway is currently protected with SpO2 of 97%, but the swelling involves the tongue, so the patient will stay under close observation with repeated airway checks. The patient and family were told clearly that the lisinopril must not be taken again and that no ACE inhibitor should be used in the future.
2. Other home medications: the list is not yet reconciled. This will be completed with the patient and pharmacy during observation and by the admitting team.

The patient understands the plan and agrees with staying for monitoring. They were told to call the nurse right away for any worsening swelling, trouble breathing, or trouble swallowing.
