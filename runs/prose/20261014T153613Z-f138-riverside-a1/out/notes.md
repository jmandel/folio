## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented on 1/21 with swelling of the lips and face that developed over several hours. There was no rash and no itching. Breathing has felt comfortable and there has been no trouble swallowing. Home medications include <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 10 mg daily, which is stopped; the patient was told not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Other home medications are hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly. Allergy list: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 13:26: BP 120/72, HR 84, RR 19, T 99 F, SpO2 96%. The patient is alert, speaking in full sentences, and handling secretions. There is visible swelling of the lips and lower face. Lungs are clear with comfortable respirations. There is no hives.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling is consistent with a reaction to the ACE inhibitor, and it is the only blood pressure medication in the history that fits this timing. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 13:45. The airway is being watched closely with repeated reassessment, and the patient will be kept under observation in the department. The patient was told the swelling can worsen and to report any change in voice, tongue swelling, or difficulty breathing right away. The ACE inhibitor stays off the medication list permanently, and this was explained to the patient.
2. Other home medications (hydrochlorothiazide and atorvastatin) have not yet been reconciled and will be addressed with the admitting or follow-up team.

Disposition will depend on how the swelling behaves over the next several hours of observation. Primary care follow-up is advised after this visit.
