## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is a middle-aged man with hypertension, diabetes and hypothyroidism who came to the ED this evening because his lips and tongue had swollen over the past several hours. He describes a thick, heavy feeling in the mouth and some trouble getting words out, but he has had no hives, no wheeze and no sense of the throat closing. He has taken a daily blood pressure pill for years. He denies any new foods, insect stings or other new exposures today. He has no known drug allergies. His other home medicines include metformin, empagliflozin, chlorthalidone, atorvastatin and levothyroxine, and these have not been reconciled in the ED.

**Exam.** Vitals at 22:40: BP 113/68, HR 95, RR 22, T 98.3 F, SpO2 94%. He is anxious but speaking in full sentences. The lips and anterior tongue are visibly swollen, the floor of the mouth is soft, and there is no stridor. Lungs are clear. There is no rash.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling is isolated to the lips and tongue without hives, which fits a bradykinin-mediated process rather than an allergic one. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 22:59. He will be kept under close observation with continuous pulse oximetry and repeated airway checks, because swelling of the tongue can progress, and we have discussed with him that we will intubate early if the airway worsens.
2. <ref id="med_lisinopril">Lisinopril 40 mg daily is stopped; he has been told not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again.</ref> This will be added to his record so that it carries forward.
3. The patient understands the plan and agrees to admission for airway observation.
