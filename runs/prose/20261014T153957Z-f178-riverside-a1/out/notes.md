## ED provider note

**Reason for visit.** Chest pain.

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department this evening with chest pain. They were uncomfortable on arrival and a little short of breath, and were placed on a monitor and evaluated promptly for a cardiac cause. There is no known drug allergy (NKDA). The home medication list includes rosuvastatin 20 mg by mouth nightly; it has not been reconciled during this visit.

**Exam.** Vitals at 22:54: BP 142/85, HR 111, RR 22, T 99.2 F, SpO2 96%. The patient was mildly tachycardic and breathing a bit fast, but speaking in full sentences and in no acute distress. Heart rhythm was regular. Lungs were clear to auscultation. Abdomen was soft and nontender.

**Results.** The ECG showed no ischemic changes. The first troponin, drawn at 23:16, was <ref id="trop_1">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The repeat troponin, drawn at 02:16, was <ref id="trop_2">Troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:16 showed sodium 140, potassium 4.5, chloride 99, bicarbonate 24, urea nitrogen 22, creatinine 0.70 with eGFR 95, and glucose 144 (H). The anion gap was 17 (H), reported by the lab as mildly elevated; the remainder of the panel is unremarkable.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The tachycardia and mild tachypnea noted on arrival were reviewed with the patient together with these reassuring results.
2. Mildly elevated glucose of 144 and anion gap of 17 are noted on the 23:16 panel, with normal bicarbonate and renal function.

The results and the reasoning behind them were discussed with the patient, who understood. They were advised to follow up with their primary care physician in the next few days and to return to the emergency department for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting.
