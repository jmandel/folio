## Emergency department provider note

**Reason for visit.** Chest pain.

**History.** The patient came to the ED on the evening of 9/20 with chest pain. The discomfort was not pleasant but was tolerable, and there was no fainting. They were seen and examined at 19:41. They have hypertension and diabetes and take metformin, lisinopril, hydrochlorothiazide and atorvastatin at home; none of these has been reconciled yet. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 19:47: BP 141/85, HR 104, RR 18, T 98.6 F, SpO2 95%. The patient is awake and conversant, in no acute distress, and speaks in full sentences. Heart rate is mildly fast with a regular rhythm. Lungs are clear on both sides. The chest wall and abdomen are soft and nontender. No leg swelling.

**Results.** The 12-lead ECG shows no ischemic changes. On the 20:12 panel, <ref id="trop_1">troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The repeat at 23:12 was also negative: <ref id="trop_2">troponin I <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. The basic metabolic panel drawn at 20:12 showed sodium 139, potassium 4.5, chloride 105, bicarbonate 24, anion gap 10, urea nitrogen 23, glucose 113 and creatinine 1.33 with an eGFR of 57. The kidney function is mildly reduced and the glucose is slightly high, but there is no acidosis or electrolyte disturbance to explain the symptoms.

**Assessment and plan**

1. Chest pain. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The pain has settled during the ED stay and the patient is comfortable at the time of this note.
2. Mildly reduced eGFR on today's panel, to be followed up by the primary care physician with a repeat chemistry panel.
3. Disposition: the patient is stable for discharge home. Return precautions were reviewed, including recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting or sweating with the pain. Follow up with the primary care physician within the week.
