## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient is seen on 3/24 for swelling of the lips and face that began earlier today. They describe a thick, heavy feeling of the lips and some tightness in the throat, without hives or itching. There was no wheeze and no trouble swallowing saliva. They have no known drug allergies on the allergy list. They take <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily, which is stopped today; the patient is instructed not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. I reviewed the reasoning with the patient: this kind of swelling can recur with any drug in the class, so the instruction is permanent.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:11: BP 146/88, HR 102, RR 24, T 98.4 F, SpO2 95%. The patient is anxious but speaking in full sentences. There is visible swelling of the lips and lower face. The tongue and floor of the mouth look normal on inspection, the voice is not muffled, and there is no stridor. Lungs are clear. There is no rash.

**Results.** No laboratory studies were needed to make the diagnosis, which rests on the history and exam.

**Course.** Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 18:30. The patient was kept in a monitored bed with continuous pulse oximetry, and I rechecked the airway and the swelling after the dose.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The timing, the absence of hives and itching, and the lisinopril exposure fit this picture. Airway remains patent for now; serial airway checks will continue while the swelling is watched. Lisinopril is stopped as above.
2. Tachycardia and mild elevation of blood pressure are most likely related to anxiety and discomfort, and will be rechecked after the swelling settles.
3. Disposition will depend on how the swelling behaves over the next hours. The patient and family understand that they should tell staff at once if breathing, swallowing, or voice changes.
