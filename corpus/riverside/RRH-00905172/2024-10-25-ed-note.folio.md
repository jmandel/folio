## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening feeling unwell, with nausea, abdominal discomfort and rapid breathing. Glucose on arrival was not markedly elevated, which prompted a closer look at the acid-base status. He takes empagliflozin at home along with metformin, glipizide, lisinopril and amlodipine. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:11: BP 116/70, HR 100, RR 19, T 99.2 F, SpO2 97%. He appears mildly dehydrated with deep, rapid respirations. Abdomen is soft.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 20:23: sodium 132, potassium 5.0, chloride 92, bicarbonate 13, anion gap 27, urea nitrogen 35, creatinine 0.93, eGFR 63, glucose 165.</ref> Venous blood gas on the same draw: pH 7.10, pCO2 28. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.6 mmol/L. This is a high anion gap metabolic acidosis with marked ketosis despite a near-normal glucose.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Euglycemic DKA, SGLT2 inhibitor-associated.</ref> The insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 21:15 and is still running. <ref id="med_empagliflozin">Empagliflozin is held.</ref>
2. Metformin is held. Lisinopril is held. Glipizide and amlodipine are continued as listed on the home medication list.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for ongoing DKA management; the admitting hospitalist has been updated.
