## Emergency department provider note

**Chief complaint.** Facial and lip swelling.

**History.** The patient arrived on the afternoon of 12/3 after noticing that their lips and the area around the mouth had become swollen and tight over the course of the day. There was no rash and no itching. They describe a thick feeling in the mouth but have been able to swallow their saliva and speak in full sentences. Their home medications include lisinopril 40 mg daily, metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly; the metformin and rosuvastatin have not yet been reconciled with the patient or pharmacy. The allergy list reads NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:01: BP 140/84, HR 82, RR 22, T 98.1 F, SpO2 96%. Comfortable, speaking clearly without stridor. Marked swelling of the lips with mild fullness of the lower face. Lungs clear. The respiratory rate is mildly elevated, so I am watching the airway closely and will recheck frequently.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The picture of swelling without hives or itching, in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor, fits this diagnosis. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started 18:20. The airway is currently patent and the oxygen saturation is acceptable; I will keep the patient under close observation and reassess the swelling and voice at intervals.
2. Home medication: <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril is stopped, and the patient was told not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. I explained that the swelling is linked to this class of drug and that it should be recorded so it is avoided in the future.
3. Metformin and rosuvastatin remain on the home list, not reconciled at this time.

The patient and family understand the plan and know to tell staff immediately if breathing or swallowing becomes harder.
