## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Weakness and several days of diarrhea.

**History.** The patient came in feeling washed out and thirsty after days of loose stools and poor intake. No chest pain, and no fainting. No known drug allergies. The home atorvastatin 40 mg nightly has not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 15:26: BP 141/85, HR 80, RR 20, T 98.4 F, SpO2 95%. Comfortable, mucous membranes slightly dry, lungs clear, abdomen soft and nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The basic metabolic panel showed sodium 133 mmol/L (L), potassium 6.3 mmol/L (HH), chloride 100, bicarbonate 16 (L), anion gap 17 (H), urea nitrogen 59 (H), creatinine 2.08 (H), eGFR 30 (L) and glucose 129 (H).</ref>

**ED course.** Given the critical potassium, calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 16:00 to 16:10 for membrane stabilization. Normal saline was started at 16:10, a 1000 mL bolus followed by 125 mL/h, and it is still running.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, most likely from volume depletion. Continue IV fluids and admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref> with potassium 6.3, treated with calcium as above. Repeat the potassium after fluids.
3. Mild anion gap acidosis with bicarbonate 16, likely from the kidney injury.

The hospitalist, Dr. Castellano, accepted the patient for admission.
