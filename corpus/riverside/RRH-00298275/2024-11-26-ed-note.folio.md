## ED provider note

**History.** The patient came to the emergency department on the evening of 11/26 with several days of cough, feeling feverish and tired, and some shortness of breath when walking. Appetite has been poor. There is no chest pain and no known sick contacts beyond a household cold. Allergies: NKDA.

**Home medications** (not yet reconciled at the time of this note): metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, dulaglutide 0.5 mg weekly, insulin glargine 14 units nightly, lisinopril 20 mg daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily, and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly.

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:18: BP 136/82, HR 97, RR 19, T 99.1 F, SpO2 94%. She is tired-appearing but conversational in full sentences. Breath sounds are diminished with crackles at the right base. Heart rhythm is regular. Abdomen is soft and nontender.

**Results.** The <ref id="cbc">11/26 20:27 blood count shows white blood cells 13.6, hemoglobin 12.3 and platelets 289</ref>. On the basic metabolic panel drawn at 20:27: sodium 137, potassium 4.8, chloride 101, bicarbonate 18 (low), anion gap 18 (high), urea nitrogen 38 (high), creatinine 2.11 (high), eGFR 38 and glucose 121.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_pneumonia">Community-acquired pneumonia</ref>, based on the cough, low-grade temperature, borderline oxygen saturation, focal crackles and raised white count. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours was started at 21:42 and is running.
2. Low bicarbonate with a raised anion gap and elevated urea nitrogen and creatinine on the 20:27 panel. Normal saline at 75 mL/h was started at 21:02 and is running. The panel will be followed.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for further treatment and monitoring.
