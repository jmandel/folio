## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented with several days of loose stools and feeling weak and run down. Home medications on the list include lisinopril 40 mg daily and chlorthalidone 12.5 mg (half of a 25 mg tablet) daily; neither has been reconciled yet in the ED. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 22:47: BP 126/76, HR 94, RR 19, T 98.7 F, SpO2 97%. Comfortable and conversant, mucous membranes somewhat dry. Abdomen soft and non-tender.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:02 showed sodium 129, potassium 6.5 (critical), chloride 106, bicarbonate 19, anion gap 4, urea nitrogen 54, creatinine 1.89, eGFR 28 and glucose 154</ref>.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.5. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given once, started 23:21 and finished 23:31, to protect the myocardium while the underlying cause is addressed.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, with urea nitrogen 54 and creatinine 1.89, most consistent with volume depletion given the history of diarrhea and the dry exam. Normal saline 1000 mL IV bolus was started at 23:31, followed by 125 mL/h, and is still running.
3. Hyponatremia, sodium 129, to be rechecked with the fluids.

The patient will be admitted to the hospitalist service for continued fluids and repeat chemistries. Home medications will be addressed by the admitting team.
