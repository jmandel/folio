## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the emergency department feeling unwell, with nausea, marked fatigue and fast breathing. They have type 2 diabetes and hypertension and take several daily medicines at home. The home medication list has not yet been reconciled; it includes <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, glipizide 5 mg daily, lisinopril 40 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily (half of a 25 mg tablet) and amlodipine 5 mg daily. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** At 21:11: BP 102/61, HR 104, RR 23, T 98.1 F, SpO2 97%. Mildly dry mucous membranes, deep and rapid respirations. Abdomen soft. Alert and conversant.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn 21:30: pH 7.15, pCO2 26. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.3 mmol/L. <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel at 21:30: sodium 133, potassium 4.7, chloride 97, bicarbonate 11, anion gap 25, urea nitrogen 33, creatinine 1.40, eGFR 57, glucose 173.</ref>

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a high anion gap acidosis with a markedly low bicarbonate and elevated ketones despite a glucose of only 173. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h is being started, with fluids, and will be started at 22:15. The patient is being admitted to the hospitalist service.
2. Home medications are not yet reconciled; the admitting team will address them.
3. Renal function: creatinine 1.40 with urea nitrogen 33, consistent with volume depletion.
