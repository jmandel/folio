## Emergency department provider note

**Reason for visit:** chest pain.

**History.** The patient is a 63-year-old who came to the ED on the evening of 1/1 with chest pain. The discomfort was described in words rather than as a severe or crushing pain, and the patient was comfortable and talking in full sentences when I first saw them at 20:12. Home medications on the list are losartan 100 mg daily, hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg nightly; none of these has been reconciled yet. Allergies: NKDA.

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:18: BP 145/87, HR 80, RR 24, T 99.1 F, SpO2 95%. The patient appeared well, not diaphoretic. Heart regular without a murmur. Lungs clear. Chest wall and abdomen were not distressing on palpation. No leg swelling.

**Results.** The ECG showed no ischemic changes. On the 20:45 panel, <ref id="trop_1">troponin I was <0.01 ng/mL</ref>, and on the repeat drawn at 23:45, <ref id="trop_2">troponin I was <0.01 ng/mL</ref>. Basic metabolic panel at 20:45: sodium 136, potassium 4.6, chloride 102, bicarbonate 26, anion gap 8, urea nitrogen 17, creatinine 1.11, eGFR 75, glucose 174 (H). The electrolytes and anion gap are unremarkable, and kidney function is normal.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_acs">Acute coronary syndrome ruled out</ref>: two troponins <0.01 three hours apart and an ECG without ischemic changes. The glucose of 174 is mildly elevated and is noted for outpatient follow-up with the patient's primary care physician.
2. Return precautions were reviewed with the patient: return for recurrent or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting or sweating. Follow-up with the primary care physician in the coming days was advised, and the patient voiced understanding.
