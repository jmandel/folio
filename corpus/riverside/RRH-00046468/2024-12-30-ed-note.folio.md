## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, abdominal discomfort and fast breathing.

**History.** The patient came in feeling unwell and short of breath, with several days of poor appetite. Home medications include <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>, metformin 1000 mg twice daily, lisinopril 40 mg daily, chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily and amlodipine 5 mg daily; none has been reconciled yet. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** At 16:01: BP 115/69, HR 87, RR 23, T 98.1 F, SpO2 94%. Breathing is deep and rapid. Mucous membranes are dry. The abdomen is soft with mild upper abdominal discomfort.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">Basic metabolic panel drawn 16:25: sodium 132, potassium 5.4, chloride 94, bicarbonate 14, anion gap 24, urea nitrogen 33, creatinine 1.18, eGFR 51, glucose 210.</ref> Venous gas drawn 16:25: pH 7.17, pCO2 21. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.7. Lipase 35.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, with a pH of 7.17, bicarbonate of 14, an anion gap of 24 and a markedly elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate despite a glucose of only 210. The insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started 17:05 and is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">Pancreatitis is being ruled out</ref> given the abdominal discomfort; the lipase of 35 is reassuring.
3. Potassium is 5.4 and will be followed closely on insulin.
