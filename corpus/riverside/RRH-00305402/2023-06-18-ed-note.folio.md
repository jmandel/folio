## ED provider note

**Chief complaint:** weakness and diarrhea.

**History.** The patient came in this afternoon after four days of loose, watery stools and poor oral intake. They felt weak and light-headed on standing and wanted to be checked. No fever, no vomiting, no chest pain, and no blood in the stool. They have no known drug allergies (NKDA). Home medicines were not yet reconciled at the time of this note.

**Exam.** Vitals at 17:40: BP 124/74, HR 85, RR 21, T 98.7 F, SpO2 99%. Appears tired but is alert and speaking in full sentences. Mucous membranes are dry. Heart rate regular, lungs clear, abdomen soft and non-tender without guarding.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The basic metabolic panel drawn at 17:58 shows sodium 129, potassium 6.3 (critical), chloride 103, bicarbonate 22, anion gap 4, urea nitrogen 34, creatinine 1.89, eGFR 32 and glucose 209.</ref> The potassium was called to me as a critical value.

**ED course.** Because of the critical potassium, I gave calcium gluconate 1 g IV, running from 18:14 to 18:24, for membrane protection. A 1000 mL bolus of normal saline was started at 18:24 and continues at 125 mL/h. The patient tolerated both without complaint.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.3, treated with IV calcium and fluids. Recheck potassium and continue cardiac monitoring.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, creatinine 1.89, most consistent with volume depletion from days of diarrhea. Continue IV fluids.
3. Hyponatremia, sodium 129, likely from the same volume loss.

The hospitalist team, Dr. Raman, was called and agreed to admit the patient for monitoring and further treatment.
