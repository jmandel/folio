## Emergency department provider note

**Chief complaint:** lip and tongue swelling.

**History.** The patient presented this evening with swelling of the lips and a sense of fullness in the tongue that has progressed over the course of the day. There was no hives, no itching and no wheeze. Swallowing is uncomfortable but they are handling their saliva and speaking in full sentences. They have taken their usual morning medications and have not eaten anything new or been exposed to anything unusual that they can identify. This has not happened before. Allergy list: no known drug allergies.

**Medications.** The home list includes metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, semaglutide 0.5 mg subcutaneously weekly and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly. These have not yet been reconciled in the department. The list also includes lisinopril 10 mg by mouth daily, addressed below.

**Exam.** Vitals at 18:47: BP 117/70, HR 105, RR 23, T 98.6 F, SpO2 95%. Uncomfortable but alert. There is visible swelling of the upper and lower lips and moderate swelling of the tongue. The voice is slightly muffled. The oropharynx is partially obscured by the tongue, with no stridor at rest. Lungs are clear. There is no rash on the skin.

**ED course.** Because of the swelling near the airway, they were placed on monitoring. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once at 19:06. The swelling and the airway are being watched closely.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. Lip and tongue swelling without hives or itching, in a patient on an ACE inhibitor, fits this diagnosis. <ref id="med_lisinopril">Lisinopril is stopped: "Do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again."</ref> The patient was told this directly. Continue close airway observation and reassess the swelling frequently.
2. The remaining home medications are listed above and still need reconciliation.
