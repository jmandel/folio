## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented this evening with swelling of the lips and face that came on over the past several hours. There was no rash and no hives. The patient reported no difficulty swallowing and no trouble breathing, and could speak in full sentences. Symptoms began without any new food, insect sting, or other clear trigger. The patient takes lisinopril 40 mg by mouth daily at home, which is the one medication on the home list that fits this picture.

**Home medications.** Lisinopril 40 mg tablet, 1 tablet by mouth daily. Metformin 1000 mg tablet, 1 tablet by mouth twice daily with meals. Empagliflozin 10 mg tablet, 1 tablet by mouth daily. Atorvastatin 20 mg tablet, 1 tablet by mouth nightly. Home medications have not yet been reconciled with the patient's pharmacy. **Allergies:** no known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 20:01: BP 120/72, HR 98, RR 17, temperature 98.6 F, SpO2 99%. Comfortable, not in distress, with no stridor and a clear voice. Visible swelling of the lips and lower face. Lungs clear bilaterally. Heart rate regular. Skin without urticaria.

**Course in the ED.** Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started at 20:20. The airway was reassessed after the dose and remained patent, with oxygen saturation holding at 99%.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The swelling without hives, in a patient on a daily ACE inhibitor, fits this diagnosis. Airway is currently stable with no stridor. Continue close observation of the airway and repeat the exam for any change in voice, breathing, or swallowing.
2. <ref id="med_lisinopril">Lisinopril is stopped. The patient was told: "Do not take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again."</ref> This was explained to the patient, who understood. Please make sure it is recorded in the chart so that it is not restarted by any future prescriber.
3. Metformin, empagliflozin, and atorvastatin are listed as home medications and are pending reconciliation.
