## ED provider note

**History.** The patient presented tonight feeling unwell, with nausea, poor appetite and marked fatigue over the past several days. They take <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref> for diabetes. Other home medications are metformin 500 mg twice daily with meals, dulaglutide 0.5 mg weekly, lisinopril 10 mg daily and rosuvastatin 20 mg nightly. No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:26: BP 98/59, HR 95, RR 17, temperature 98.5 F, SpO2 94%. The patient looks tired and dry, with deep, somewhat labored breathing. The abdomen is soft with mild diffuse discomfort.

**Results.** The <ref id="bmp">basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:54 shows sodium 130, potassium 5.5, chloride 94, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 28, urea nitrogen 24, creatinine 1.91, eGFR 28 and glucose 183</ref>. The venous blood gas at 23:54 shows pH 7.09 with pCO2 30. Beta-hydroxybutyrate is 4.9 mmol/L.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>: a severe anion gap acidosis with ketones markedly elevated while glucose is only 183, in a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor. An insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 00:30 and is running. Admit to the hospitalist service.
2. Abdominal discomfort: <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">pancreatitis is being ruled out</ref> as a cause of the nausea and pain.
3. Potassium and creatinine are elevated on the labs above, likely from volume depletion. Repeat chemistry will guide fluids and potassium.
4. Home medications have not yet been reconciled; the admitting team will address which to hold.
