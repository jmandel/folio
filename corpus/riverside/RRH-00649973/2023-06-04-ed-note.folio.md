## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented to the ED at 14:34 with swelling of the lips and face that came on over the past several hours. There was no rash, no hives, and no difficulty swallowing. Voice quality was normal and there was no sense of throat tightness. At home the patient takes <ref id="med_lisinopril">lisinopril 10 mg by mouth daily, which is now stopped; the patient was told not to take lisinopril or any ACE inhibitor again</ref>. Other home medications are amlodipine 5 mg daily and atorvastatin 40 mg nightly, listed here but not reconciled during this visit.

**Allergies.** No known drug allergies.

**Exam.** Vitals at 14:40: BP 129/77, HR 87, RR 19, T 98.3 F, SpO2 98%. The patient was comfortable, speaking in full sentences, and not in distress. There was mild swelling of the lips without stridor or drooling. The oropharynx was clear on inspection, the tongue was not enlarged, and lungs were clear bilaterally. The skin showed no urticaria.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_angioedema">Angioedema, ACE inhibitor-associated</ref>. The picture is typical: swelling of the lips and face without hives or itching, in a patient taking an ACE inhibitor. The airway is currently patent and oxygen saturation is normal. Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV was given once, started at 14:59. We will keep the patient under observation in the department with repeat airway checks, and we will reassess for any progression of swelling, voice change, or trouble breathing.
2. Medication safety: the patient and family were counseled about the ACE inhibitor and that this reaction can recur if the drug is taken again. The primary care physician should be informed of the reaction so that another blood pressure medicine can be chosen.
3. Disposition will depend on how the swelling behaves over the period of observation. The patient was told to call for help at once if swelling spreads to the tongue or throat.
