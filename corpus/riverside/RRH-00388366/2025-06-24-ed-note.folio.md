## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, malaise and rapid breathing.

**History.** The patient with type 2 diabetes came in feeling weak and nauseated, with fast breathing over the past day. They take empagliflozin at home; <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily was listed but not yet reconciled</ref> at the time of this note. Allergies: NKDA. The remaining home medication list has not yet been reconciled.

**Exam.** Vitals at 16:01: BP 100/60, HR 104, RR 22, T 99.2 F, SpO2 97%. Ill-appearing but alert, dry mucous membranes, deep rapid respirations. Abdomen soft, without focal tenderness. No focal neurologic findings.

**Results.** Venous blood gas drawn 16:17 showed pH 7.08 and pCO2 27. Beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.6. <ref id="bmp">BMP drawn 16:17: sodium 134, potassium 5.3, chloride 94, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 32, urea nitrogen 35, creatinine 1.57, eGFR 50, glucose 242.</ref>

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, with a severe anion gap metabolic acidosis, ketonemia and a glucose of only 242. This fits SGLT2 inhibitor-associated ketoacidosis. Started an insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h, with dextrose-containing fluids because glucose is not markedly elevated.
2. Acute kidney injury, mild, with creatinine 1.57 and potassium 5.3, likely from volume depletion. Fluids as above and repeat chemistries.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for ongoing DKA management; discussed with the admitting physician.
