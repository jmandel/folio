## Emergency department provider note

**History.** The patient presented with several days of feeling run down and weak, with little to drink. There is no chest pain and no loss of consciousness. Home medications on the list are metformin 1000 mg twice daily with meals, losartan 50 mg daily and hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily; none has been reconciled yet in the ED. Allergy: lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** Vitals at 23:01: BP 123/74, HR 90, RR 23, temperature 98.6 F, SpO2 96%. The patient is alert and conversant, appears mildly dry, and is breathing comfortably on room air. Abdomen is soft and nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:17 shows sodium 131 (L), potassium 6.8 (HH), chloride 101, bicarbonate 22, anion gap 8, urea nitrogen 52 (H), creatinine 2.89 (H), eGFR 24 and glucose 112 (H)</ref>. The potassium is critically high and the renal function is markedly reduced.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref> with potassium 6.8. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given once, started 23:35 and finished 23:45, for membrane stabilization.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref> with creatinine 2.89 and urea nitrogen 52. Normal saline 1000 mL bolus was started at 23:45, followed by 125 mL/h, and is still running.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for continued fluids and monitoring of potassium and creatinine.
