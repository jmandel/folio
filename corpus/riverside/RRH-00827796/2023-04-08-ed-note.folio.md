## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Weakness and several days of diarrhea.

**History.** The patient came to the ED on the evening of 4/8 feeling weak and lightheaded after about four days of loose, frequent stools with poor intake. There has been no fever and no chest pain. Home medications are on the list but have not yet been reconciled, and the allergy list shows lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** At 23:01: BP 123/74, HR 90, RR 23, T 98.6 F, SpO2 96%. The patient looks tired and mildly dry, with dry mucous membranes. Heart rhythm is regular, lungs are clear, and the abdomen is soft and nontender.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The basic metabolic panel drawn at 23:17 shows sodium 131 (L), potassium 6.8 (HH), chloride 101, bicarbonate 22, anion gap 8, urea nitrogen 52 (H), creatinine 2.89 (H), eGFR 24 (L), glucose 112 (H).</ref> The potassium was called as a critical value.

**ED course.** Calcium gluconate 1 g IV was given from 23:35 to 23:45 for the critical potassium. A normal saline 1000 mL bolus was started at 23:45, followed by 125 mL/h, and is still running.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_hyperk">Hyperkalemia</ref>, potassium 6.8, treated with IV calcium as above. Repeat chemistry will follow after fluids.
2. <ref id="dx_aki">Acute kidney injury</ref>, creatinine 2.89 with urea nitrogen 52, most consistent with volume depletion from the diarrhea. Continue IV fluids.
3. Admit to the hospitalist service for further treatment and monitoring.
