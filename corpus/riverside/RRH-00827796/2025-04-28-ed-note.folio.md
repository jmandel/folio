## ED provider note

**Chief complaint.** Nausea, weakness and shortness of breath.

**History.** The patient has diabetes and arrived with several hours of nausea, fatigue and rapid breathing. Home medications are listed but have not yet been reconciled, including <ref id="med_empagliflozin">empagliflozin 10 mg daily</ref>. The allergy list shows lisinopril (cough).

**Exam.** At 14:18: BP 110/66, HR 89, RR 24, T 98 F, SpO2 94%. The patient is breathing deeply and has dry mucous membranes. The abdomen is soft.

**Results.** <ref id="bmp">The basic metabolic panel at 14:28 shows sodium 132 (L), potassium 4.8, chloride 94 (L), bicarbonate 11 (LL), anion gap 27 (H), urea nitrogen 26 (H), creatinine 1.85 (H), eGFR 40 (L), glucose 192 (H).</ref> Venous blood gas pH 7.17 (LL), pCO2 21 (L). Beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.2.

**ED course.** The insulin infusion per DKA protocol at 0.1 units/kg/h was started at 15:22.

**Assessment and plan**

1. <ref id="dx_dka">Diabetic ketoacidosis, euglycemic</ref>, with a wide anion gap, pH 7.17 and beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.2 despite a glucose of 192. Insulin infusion as above; admit to the hospitalist service.
2. <ref id="dx_pancreatitis">R/O pancreatitis</ref> given the nausea; lipase is pending.
