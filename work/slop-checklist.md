# Slop checklist for site copy

Sources:
- Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing
- Pangram Labs, "9 Signs of AI Writing, Backed by Data": https://www.pangram.com/signs-of-ai-writing
- tropes.fyi catalogue of LLM writing tropes: https://tropes.fyi/tropes-md (directory: https://tropes.fyi/directory)
- Blake Stockton, "Don't Write Like AI": colons https://www.blakestockton.com/colons-everywhere/ and red-flag phrases https://www.blakestockton.com/red-flag-phrases/

Each marker gives an example and a test. A hit means rewrite or cut. Cutting is usually better than rephrasing.

| # | Marker | Example | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Counts as the point of a heading or sentence ("compulsive counting") | "One fact in four records", "Four systems, four shapes", "in one of two ways" | Remove the number. If the heading loses nothing a reader needs, say what the section is about instead. |
| 2 | Rule of three, by default | "slow, drops local detail, and separates…"; three-part captions | Are there really three things, and does the reader need all three? Otherwise cut to the ones that matter, or use a sentence. |
| 3 | False contrast / "not just X but Y" | "not X, but Y"; "isn't A, it's B"; "Y rather than X" | Did anyone claim X? If not, delete X and state Y. |
| 4 | Negative parallelism / dramatic countdown | "No AI. No network. Just code." | State the positive fact once. |
| 5 | Em-dash rhythm | clauses spliced with dashes | Use a period, comma or parentheses. |
| 6 | Throat-clearing intro / summarizing outro | "Here is how…", "In short…", a closing line that restates the section | Delete; start with the first fact. |
| 7 | Vague grandiosity / AI vocabulary | seamless, powerful, unlock, at its core, here's the thing, key, crucial, showcase, underscore, testament | Replace with the concrete claim or delete. |
| 8 | Signposting / teacher voice | "Let's", "Here is how", "It's worth noting", "Start here" when not needed | Delete the signpost. |
| 9 | Hedged mush | "can help", "in some ways", "aims to" | Say what it does, or say it isn't built yet. |
| 10 | Tidy aphorisms / quotable one-liners | "A note and its data are one record"; "FHIR is one view" as a slogan | Would an engineer write it in a design doc? If it reads like a tagline, replace with the fact. |
| 11 | Colon-reveal and "The X?" headings or sentences | "The idea", "The problem", "Mapper: written once"; "The result? …" | Heading names the content; sentence states the fact without a reveal. |
| 12 | Title-case or label-like headings as filler | "The problem", "The idea" | Name what the section contains. |
| 13 | Bold-lead bullets everywhere | **Label:** explanation, in every item | Use plain sentences or a table. |
| 14 | First sentence restates the heading | H: "Sharing part of a record" / P: "Linda shares part of her record…" | Start with what the heading doesn't already say. |
| 15 | Copula avoidance / "serves as" | "serves as", "stands as", "features" | Use "is" / "has". |
| 16 | Comma-clipped trailing phrase | "…in their own shape, signed." | Make the tail its own clause or fold it in. |
| 17 | Superficial -ing tails | "…, highlighting how…" | Delete. |
| 18 | Promise of revelation | "now reads differently", "see a planted trap" as a tease | Say what changes. |
